## What does a Sydney app development company actually build?

**Most apps commissioned by Sydney businesses fall into three groups: apps for customers, apps for staff, and mobile companions to an existing web platform.** Each has a different cost profile and a different definition of success, so it helps to know which one you're buying before you talk to developers.

| Type of app | Typical Sydney examples | What usually drives the cost |
|---|---|---|
| Customer app | Bookings and memberships, ordering, loyalty, account self service, client portals for professional firms | Design polish, payments, notifications, app store review |
| Staff and field app | Job sheets for trades and facilities crews, inspections and audits, delivery and logistics, rostering | Offline mode, photo and signature capture, sync with a job or ERP system |
| Companion app | A mobile front end for a SaaS product or internal web system that already exists | Reusing the existing API safely, matching permissions, keeping two clients in step |

In every group, the phone app is only part of the work. A business app nearly always needs a backend (the database, business rules and logins) and a web admin portal so your team can manage users, content and orders. When two quotes look wildly different, the gap is usually here: one includes the backend and admin portal, the other quietly assumes you already have them.

Field apps deserve a specific mention for Sydney. Crews working in basements, underground car parks, new building shells or regional NSW sites lose signal regularly, so an app that needs a live connection to save a job sheet will fail in exactly the places it's used. Offline storage with reliable sync is a design decision to make at the start, not a feature to add later.

## How do you choose the best app developers in Sydney?

**Choose on evidence you can check: apps they have shipped, how they scope the parts you can't see, and what they hand over at the end.** Sydney's app market runs from solo freelancers to large digital agencies, and the right choice depends on the project rather than the size of the firm.

A checklist you can use on any shortlist:

1. **Download their apps.** Ask for two or three live apps similar to yours and install them. Check how they handle a weak connection, a login reset and a slow screen.
2. **Ask who will do the work.** Find out who designs, who builds, who tests, and whether the people you meet are the people on the project.
3. **Read the scope, not the price.** A credible scope lists the backend, admin portal, integrations, testing devices and app store submission as separate lines.
4. **Check account ownership.** The app should be published under your organisation's Apple and Google accounts, with the code repository and cloud hosting in your name.
5. **Check the IP clause.** The contract should say when the code and designs are assigned to you, and list any of the developer's own tools that are licensed rather than assigned.
6. **Ask about life after launch.** Apple and Google ship major operating system releases every year. Ask what the warranty covers and what ongoing support costs.
7. **Call a past client.** Ask what went wrong and how the developer handled it. Every project has a hard moment; the answer tells you more than any portfolio.

Be fair to the alternatives. A capable freelancer can be the right hire for a small, well defined app if you're willing to manage the project and the backend yourself. A large agency suits a consumer app with a marketing launch and a big brand budget. ## Flutter, native or a web app: which does a Sydney business need?

**For most business apps, Flutter is the best value, because one codebase builds both the iOS and Android apps.** Flutter's own documentation describes it as a way to build multiplatform apps from a single codebase. AppGurus estimates cross platform builds with Flutter or React Native save 30 to 40% compared with two fully native apps, which means native typically costs around 1.4 to 1.7 times as much for the app itself.

| If your app... | Usually the better choice |
|---|---|
| Needs the same features on iOS and Android, a custom design, and offline data | Flutter |
| Shares a lot of code with an existing React web app and team | React Native |
| Depends on new Apple or Google features on release day, or deep hardware access | Native Swift and Kotlin |
| Is an internal tool used on known devices, with forms and content | A responsive web app, no store needed |
| Mostly shows information and needs to be found in Google | A website, not an app |

The saving applies to the app on the phone, not the whole project: the backend and admin portal cost the same whichever approach you choose. ## Who should own the app store accounts and the code?

**Your organisation should own the Apple and Google developer accounts, the code repository and the hosting, with your developer added as a user.** It's a common problem when a business changes developers: the app is live, but it sits in a former developer's personal account.

Practical points that catch people out:

- **Apple needs a D-U-N-S number.** To enrol an organisation in the Apple Developer Program, Apple requires a legal entity and a D-U-N-S number from Dun & Bradstreet. Trading names aren't accepted. Request one early, because it can hold up a launch.
- **Staff apps can stay private.** An app for your own team doesn't need a public listing. Apple distributes custom apps to an organisation through Apple Business Manager, and managed Google Play supports private apps distributed only to your staff.
- **Apps can be moved.** If an existing app sits in someone else's account, Apple and Google both support transferring it to yours, keeping the listing, ratings and reviews. The current account holder has to start or approve the transfer, so ask for it before the relationship ends.

## Building apps for NSW Government and regulated Sydney sectors

**NSW Government agencies must generally buy ICT through the ICT Services Scheme on buy.nsw, unless another mandated whole of government arrangement applies.** Registered suppliers on the scheme can take low risk contracts up to $150,000 ex GST, and Advanced suppliers can take higher risk contracts above that. We're not on the scheme at the time of writing, so an agency would engage us through a supplier that is.

Private sector apps in health, finance and education carry their own obligations. A health app that collects patient information in NSW brings in state health privacy law as well as the Commonwealth Privacy Act, and payment features bring in your payment provider's security requirements. Those shape the design: what's stored on the phone, how long it's kept, and who can see it.
