## Where does AI save time in a small business?

**AI saves the most time on reading and writing work: replying, drafting, summarising and tidying up information.** That is most of the admin load in a small business, and today's general AI assistants handle it well with a person checking the output.

The Australian Government's AI Adoption Tracker, which surveys over 400 businesses each month, reported in June 2025 that 41% of small and medium enterprises were adopting AI, with services and retail the highest adopters. The same release said the share of businesses unaware of how to use AI had fallen to 21%.

| Area of the business | What AI does well today | What still needs a person |
|---|---|---|
| Enquiries and email | Drafting replies, sorting enquiries, summarising long threads | Anything that commits you to a price, a date or a promise |
| Quotes and proposals | First drafts from your notes and past quotes, tidying scope wording | Pricing, margins and final sign off |
| Admin | Meeting notes, task lists, letters, simple policies and procedures | Checking facts, names and figures |
| Bookkeeping preparation | Categorising transactions, explaining spreadsheet formulas, summarising supplier invoices | Tax decisions and lodgement, which stay with your accountant |
| Marketing | Social posts, newsletters, product descriptions, ad variations | Brand voice, claims and anything regulated |
| Hiring | Job ads, interview questions, summarising applications | Decisions about people |
| Customer service | Help articles and canned replies; chatbots on your website | Complaints, refunds and anything sensitive |

A useful rule: if a task starts with "read this and..." or "write a first draft of...", AI can probably help now. If it starts with "decide whether...", keep a person in charge.

## Which AI tools should a small business start with?

**Start with the AI already inside the software you pay for, then add one general assistant if you need more.** Adding three new tools at once makes it hard to tell what is working.

At the time of writing (November 2026):

- **Google Workspace.** Google lists Gemini as included in its business plans: in Gmail on Business Starter (listed at AU$9.90 per user a month) and across Gmail, Docs, Meet and more on Business Standard (AU$19.80) and Plus (AU$30.90).
- **Microsoft 365.** Microsoft offers Copilot Business as an add on for organisations with up to 300 users on eligible Microsoft 365 Business plans, with an annual commitment. Check Microsoft's current pricing for your plan.
- **ChatGPT or Claude business plans.** Both offer team workspaces with admin controls. Claude's Team plan is listed at US$20 per seat per month on annual billing (US$25 monthly). For eight staff on annual billing that is US$160 a month, or about A$229 at the Reserve Bank's rate of roughly US$0.70 to the dollar.

## Is it safe to put business data into AI tools?

**It depends on the plan and the data: keep personal and sensitive information out of free and personal AI accounts, and check the data terms of any business plan before staff use it.** The OAIC's guidance on commercially available AI products, published in October 2024, sets out the position for businesses covered by the Privacy Act.

Its key points, in plain terms:

- Privacy obligations apply to personal information you put into an AI system and to the output it generates.
- If AI creates or infers information about a person, that counts as collecting personal information.
- You can generally use personal information only for the purpose you collected it for, unless an exception applies.
- The OAIC recommends that organisations do not enter personal information, and particularly sensitive information, into publicly available generative AI tools.

Many small businesses with annual turnover of $3 million or less are exempt from the Privacy Act, but the OAIC lists exceptions, including health service providers and businesses that trade in personal information. Even if you're exempt, your customers will expect the same care, and larger customers often require it in their contracts. Our [Privacy Act and AI guide](/guides/privacy-act-and-ai) goes deeper.

A simple staff rule works well: no customer names, contact details, health, financial or staff records in any AI tool that isn't on the approved list.

## When is a custom AI tool worth it?

**A custom AI tool is worth it when you have a repeated task, done many times a week, that general assistants can't do well because it needs your data, your rules or your systems.** Otherwise, subscriptions are cheaper and improve every few months without you paying for it.

Signs a custom tool may pay off:

- Staff copy information from one system into an AI assistant and then back into another system, every day.
- The task depends on your own documents, price lists or past jobs, and accuracy matters.
- The output needs to land in your CRM, job software or accounting system automatically.
- Volume is high enough that saving even ten minutes per job adds up to real hours each week.

Signs it won't:

- The task happens a few times a month.
- Nobody can describe what a good result looks like.
- The underlying process is still changing.

Often the right first step isn't AI at all but a simple automation between two systems. Our [AI agents vs chatbots vs automation guide](/guides/ai-agents-vs-chatbots-vs-automation) helps sort out which you need.

## What does AI cost a small business?

**Subscriptions cost tens of dollars per person per month; custom AI starts in the tens of thousands of dollars.** Here is how the options compare.

| Option | What it is | Typical cost |
|---|---|---|
| AI inside existing software | Gemini in Google Workspace, Copilot in Microsoft 365 | Included in some plans, or an add on per user per month |
| General AI assistant, business plan | ChatGPT or Claude team workspace | Tens of dollars per person per month |
| Automation with AI steps | A tool like n8n, Make or Zapier calling an AI model | Platform fees plus a build; see our [workflow automation cost guide](/guides/workflow-automation-cost) |
| Website chatbot | Off the shelf or custom | See our [AI chatbot cost guide](/guides/ai-chatbot-cost-australia) |
| Custom AI tool or agent | Software built around your data and systems | See our [AI agent cost guide](/guides/ai-agent-cost-australia) and [AI development cost guide](/guides/ai-development-cost-australia) |

For a custom build, also count the monthly running cost of the AI model and hosting, and the time your team spends testing it.

## What free and low cost help does the government offer?

**The best starting point is AI.gov.au, which the National AI Centre launched in May 2026 to bring the government's AI guidance and resources for businesses into one place.** Its first release focuses on SMEs and not for profits.

The Department of Industry, Science and Resources says AI.gov.au includes:

- the Guidance for AI Adoption, which we explain in our [Guidance for AI Adoption guide](/guides/guidance-for-ai-adoption);
- guidance on being clear about content generated by AI;
- AI skills for business courses;
- an AI event calendar;
- a directory of Australian AI businesses;
- the AI Adoption Tracker.

For funding, the business.gov.au grants and programs finder lets you filter by state, industry and an AI assistance focus, and shows whether each program is open. Programs open and close often, so check on the day.

## A 30 day plan to try AI safely

**Pick one tool, one or two tasks and one person to own the trial, and measure the time saved before you spend more.**

1. **Days 1 to 3: choose.** Pick the AI in a tool you already pay for, or one business plan. Write a one page rule: which tool, which tasks, and what data must never go in.
2. **Days 4 to 7: pick two tasks.** Choose weekly tasks that are mostly writing or summarising, such as enquiry replies and quote drafts. Note how long each takes today.
3. **Week 2: try it on real work.** Use the tool on every instance of those tasks. A person checks every output before it goes anywhere.
4. **Week 3: write down what works.** Save the prompts and examples that give good results so others can reuse them.
5. **Week 4: measure and decide.** Compare time spent with week 1 and note any errors caught. Keep it, change the tasks, or stop.

If the trial shows a task where AI clearly helps but the general tools keep falling short, that is the point to talk to someone about a custom tool.
