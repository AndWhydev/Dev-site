## How much does a custom CRM cost in Australia?

**A custom CRM built by an Australian team usually costs between $20,000 and $250,000 (AUD, ex GST), and the band is set by integrations, data migration and permissions rather than by the number of screens.** A CRM that tracks contacts and a pipeline for one sales team sits at the bottom. A CRM that runs quoting, jobs, a customer portal and a two way accounting sync sits near the top.

| CRM type | Typical scope | Market range (AUD, ex GST) |
|---|---|---|
| Simple CRM for one team | Contacts, companies, a single pipeline, notes, tasks, basic reports | $20,000 to $40,000 |
| CRM with automation | Several pipelines, email and SMS automation, quoting, one or two integrations such as accounting or calendars | $40,000 to $100,000 |
| Operations CRM or industry system | Several teams with different permissions, job or case management, a customer portal, several integrations, audit trails | $100,000 to $250,000+ |

These bands combine PerfectionGeeks' tiers ($20k to $40k basic, $40k to $80k with automation, $80k to $150k+ enterprise), Appinventiv's 2026 Australian range of $40,000 to $250,000, and Expeed's general custom software bands of $15k to $40k for simple internal tools and $40k to $150k for mid complexity systems. Appinventiv also shows how industry changes the number: it puts real estate CRMs at $60,000 to $140,000 and finance CRMs at $90,000 to $250,000.

The ranges are market ranges, not quotes. Add GST, and budget roughly 15 to 20% of the build cost each year for maintenance, plus hosting. Our [custom software cost guide](/guides/custom-software-development-cost-australia) explains the general arithmetic behind these bands.

## When is HubSpot, Salesforce, Zoho or GoHighLevel enough?

**An off the shelf CRM is enough when your process looks like a normal sales or service pipeline and your team is small enough that seat fees stay modest.** That describes most small businesses. Try one before you commission a build.

| Platform | Best fit | Pricing model at the time of writing (November 2026) | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| HubSpot | Marketing led businesses that want forms, email, landing pages and a pipeline together | Per seat. Sales Hub Professional is listed at US$90 a seat a month on annual billing, with a one time US$1,500 onboarding fee | Prices step up sharply between tiers; contact tiers and add ons change the total |
| Salesforce | Larger or more complex sales teams, multi step approvals, deep reporting | Per user. The Australian page lists Starter Suite at AU$35 and Pro Suite at AU$140 a user a month, billed annually | Implementation partners and admin time often cost more than the licences |
| Zoho CRM | Cost conscious teams that want a broad feature set and other Zoho apps | Per user, across Standard, Professional, Enterprise and Ultimate editions; a free edition covers up to 3 users. Zoho says local taxes such as GST are added | Check which edition includes the automation and API limits you need |
| GoHighLevel | Businesses that run on calls, SMS, bookings and follow up, and agencies managing client accounts | Per account, not per user. Starter is listed at US$97 a month with unlimited users and contacts | Usage charges for phone numbers, SMS, email and AI are on top of the plan |

Prices change often, so always check the vendor's live pricing page before you budget. Converted figures on this page use the Reserve Bank's rate of about US$0.70 to the Australian dollar at the time of writing.

Choose off the shelf when:

- your sales process has standard stages and one or two pipelines;
- you can accept the vendor's data model and reporting;
- integrations you need already exist in the platform's marketplace;
- you want to be running next month, not next quarter.

## What does a CRM cost over five years: subscription or custom?

**For a team of about 20, a subscription CRM is usually cheaper over five years. As the team grows, per seat fees grow with it while a custom system's running cost barely moves.** The table below uses list prices at the time of writing and the custom build from the worked example further down. It is illustrative arithmetic, not a quote.

| Option | Users | Five year arithmetic | Five year total (approx. AUD, before GST) |
|---|---|---|---|
| GoHighLevel Starter | Unlimited | US$97 × 60 months = US$5,820 | $8,300, plus usage charges |
| HubSpot Sales Hub Professional | 20 | US$90 × 20 seats × 60 months + US$1,500 onboarding = US$109,500 | $156,400 |
| Salesforce Pro Suite | 20 | AU$140 × 20 × 60 months | $168,000 |
| Custom CRM (worked example) | Any | $125,400 build + 5 × $23,610 running | $243,450 |
| HubSpot Sales Hub Professional | 60 | US$90 × 60 × 60 months + US$1,500 = US$325,500 | $465,000 |
| Salesforce Pro Suite | 60 | AU$140 × 60 × 60 months | $504,000 |

Three things the table doesn't show:

- **Setup and admin for subscriptions.** Configuring a platform, migrating data and paying an administrator or partner can add tens of thousands of dollars, especially on Salesforce.
- **Workarounds.** Staff time lost to re-keying, spreadsheets on the side and manual follow ups is a real cost if the platform doesn't fit.
- **Lock in and exit.** Leaving a subscription CRM means exporting data and rebuilding automations elsewhere. With custom software you own the code once it is paid for, but you are responsible for maintaining it.

So the honest reading: at 20 users with a standard process, buy. At 60 or more users, or when the CRM runs your operations, a custom build can pay for itself, and the decision turns on fit rather than price.

## What drives the cost of a custom CRM?

**The biggest cost drivers are integrations, migration and the number of roles with different permissions.** Each adds design, build and testing work to every feature.

| Driver | Lower cost | Higher cost |
|---|---|---|
| Users and roles | One team, everyone sees everything | Sales, service, finance, managers and customers, each with different access |
| Pipelines and records | One pipeline, standard contact and company records | Several pipelines, custom records such as jobs, properties, policies or sites |
| Integrations | None, or one well documented API | Accounting, email, telephony, calendars, ad platforms, an ERP, two way sync |
| Automation | Simple reminders | Branching sequences, approvals, SLA timers, lead routing rules |
| Reporting | A few fixed reports | Dashboards by team and user, forecasts, exports for finance |
| Mobile use | Responsive web screens | An app for field staff that works offline |
| Data migration | Starting fresh | Years of records from spreadsheets and an old CRM, needing deduplication |
| Compliance | Low sensitivity contact data | Health, financial or legal data with audit logs and tighter access control |

## What are the signs you've outgrown an off the shelf CRM?

**You've outgrown a CRM when the team spends more effort working around it than working in it.** One or two of these is normal; four or more suggests it's time to price the alternatives.

- Staff keep a spreadsheet beside the CRM because the CRM can't hold the information they need.
- The same customer details are typed into the CRM, the accounting system and a job tool.
- You pay for a higher tier just to get one feature, such as custom objects or API access.
- Reports are built by exporting to Excel every week.
- Automations break whenever someone changes a field, and nobody knows how they work.
- The vendor's next price rise would cost more per year than a meaningful part of a build.
- Customers ask for a portal or a self service view the platform can't provide.

## Should you extend the CRM you already have instead?

**Often, yes: keeping your current CRM and building custom integrations or screens around it is the cheapest way to fix a poor fit.** It suits businesses whose CRM handles contacts and email well but can't talk to the rest of the business.

Common extensions:

- a sync between the CRM and Xero or MYOB so invoices and payments show against the customer;
- a custom quoting tool that writes deals back to the CRM;
- lead routing rules the platform can't express;
- a customer portal that reads from the CRM;
- automations through a platform like n8n, Make or Zapier, compared in our [automation tools guide](/guides/n8n-vs-make-vs-zapier).

The trade off: you keep paying the subscription and you work within the platform's API limits and data model. If the extensions keep growing, that is a sign the platform no longer fits.

## A worked example: a custom CRM for a 20 person service business

**This is a hypothetical business with illustrative figures inside the published ranges.** A 20 person service company wants one system for enquiries, quotes, jobs and follow up, connected to its accounting software, replacing a basic CRM and several spreadsheets.

1. **Estimate hours by feature.** Contacts and companies 60 hours; enquiry pipeline and job records 80; quoting with templates 70; two way accounting sync 100; email and SMS integration 50; dashboards and reports 60; roles and permissions 40; migrating records from the old CRM and spreadsheets 60; interface design 60; automated testing and QA 90; deployment and environments 30; project management 60. Total: 760 hours.
2. **Apply an assumed market blended rate** of $165 an hour (not our rate): 760 × $165 = $125,400 ex GST.
3. **Add running costs.** Maintenance at 15% of the build is $18,810 a year; hosting, email and SMS services at an assumed $400 a month is $4,800. Total: $23,610 a year, plus SMS usage.
4. **Five year total:** $125,400 + (5 × $23,610) = $243,450 ex GST.

Against a subscription at 20 seats, that is more expensive, so the build only makes sense here if the quoting, job and accounting workflow is what the business actually needs and the platforms can't do it without heavy workarounds. If the company expects to double its headcount, or the system replaces several other subscriptions, the comparison moves in the build's favour.

## How do you migrate contacts and history without losing data?

**Plan the migration as its own piece of work: map every field, clean the data before you move it, and run the old and new systems side by side for a short period.** Migration is where CRM projects most often slip.

1. **Export everything early**, including notes, activity history, attachments and custom fields, not just contacts.
2. **Map fields** from the old structure to the new one and decide what to drop.
3. **Deduplicate and fix** records before import. Doing this in house is cheaper than paying developers to do it.
4. **Keep consent records.** Who agreed to receive marketing, when and how must come across with the contact.
5. **Do a trial import**, check a sample by hand, then do the final import.
6. **Freeze the old system** to read only once the new one is live, rather than deleting it straight away.

## What Australian rules must a CRM support?

**Your CRM holds personal information and sends marketing, so it has to support the Privacy Act and the Spam Act whichever option you choose.** This is general information, not legal advice.

- **Spam Act 2003.** The ACMA says you need consent before sending marketing emails or SMS, you must identify yourself as the sender, and every message must make it easy to unsubscribe. It also says it is up to you to prove consent, so record who gave it, when and how.
- **Privacy Act 1988, APP 11.** If your business is covered by the Act, you must take reasonable steps to protect personal information from misuse, interference, loss and unauthorised access, and destroy or de-identify it when it is no longer needed. In a CRM that means sensible roles, audit logs, backups and a retention rule.
- **Where the data is stored.** Check which region a subscription CRM hosts your data in. A custom CRM can be hosted in an Australian cloud region by default.
