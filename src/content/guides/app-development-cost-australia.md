## What does a business app cost to build in Australia?

**Most business apps built by Australian teams cost between $50,000 and $250,000 (AUD, ex GST), with simple apps from about $20,000 and enterprise apps above $350,000.** The range is wide because "an app" can mean a single-purpose tool with no backend or a platform that connects staff, customers and three internal systems.

Consumer app guides dominate search results, but business apps have a different cost profile. They usually have fewer screens and less marketing polish, and far more integration, security and admin work. This guide focuses on apps that companies build for their staff, customers or partners.

| Business app type | What it usually includes | Typical range (AUD, ex GST) |
|---|---|---|
| Single-purpose staff tool | Checklists, forms, photo capture, sync to one system | $30,000 to $70,000 |
| Customer self-service app | Login, account details, bookings or orders, notifications, payments | $60,000 to $150,000 |
| Field service or operations app | Job lists, offline mode, signatures, photos, two-way sync with a job or ERP system | $90,000 to $200,000 |
| Two-sided platform | Marketplace or on-demand service with separate apps or roles for each side | $130,000 to $300,000 |
| Enterprise app | SSO, device management, audit logging, several integrations, security review | $200,000 to $350,000+ |

These ranges draw on AppGurus' 2026 guide (standard business apps $50k to $120k, feature-rich platforms $120k to $250k, enterprise $200k to $350k+, business productivity tools $40k to $120k) and Wave Digital's July 2026 guide, which puts most production-ready apps from full-service teams at $125k to $250k or more.

## Why do Australian app cost guides disagree?

**They describe different deliverables.** AppGurus starts simple apps and MVPs at $20,000 to $50,000. Wave Digital says anything under $75,000 is usually a prototype or proof of concept with limited production readiness, and that most production-ready MVPs cost $125,000 to $250,000.

Both can be right. The lower figure fits a focused app with a small feature set, standard design and a lightweight backend. The higher figure includes product strategy, user research, bespoke design, a production backend and ongoing release management. When comparing quotes, ask which of those each one includes. ## Where does the money go in an app project?

**The phone app is the visible part, but the backend, admin tools and integrations often cost as much or more.** AppGurus estimates the backend alone at $10,000 to $20,000 for simple apps and $30,000 to $80,000 for complex or marketplace apps.

| Component | What it covers | Share of a typical business app budget |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery and UX design | Workflows, user journeys, clickable prototype, design system | 10 to 15% |
| Mobile app (iOS and Android) | Screens, navigation, offline storage, device features, notifications | 30 to 40% |
| Backend and API | Database, business logic, authentication, file storage | 20 to 25% |
| Admin web portal | Staff tools to manage users, content, orders and reports | 8 to 15% |
| Integrations | CRM, ERP, accounting, payments, identity provider | 10 to 20% |
| Testing and release | Device testing, beta programs, store submission, monitoring setup | 8 to 12% |

These shares are typical patterns rather than fixed rules. An app with no integrations shifts budget to design; an app that syncs with a legacy ERP can spend a quarter of its budget on that one connection.

## Flutter, React Native or native: how does the choice affect cost?

**For most business apps, a cross-platform framework is the cheaper option because one codebase serves both iOS and Android.** AppGurus estimates cross-platform builds with Flutter or React Native save 30 to 40% compared with two fully native apps.

| Approach | Relative cost of the app itself | Best fit | When it's the wrong choice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flutter | Baseline (one codebase) | Business apps with custom UI, consistent look on both platforms, offline data | Apps that must adopt new iOS features on day one |
| React Native | Similar to Flutter | Teams that already work in React and TypeScript, apps sharing code with a React web app | Apps with heavy custom animation or graphics |
| Native iOS and Android (Swift and Kotlin) | About 1.4 to 1.7 times the cross-platform cost | Apps relying on advanced hardware, background processing or platform-specific features | Budget-constrained business apps with standard features |
| Progressive web app | Often lower again | Internal tools used on known devices, content and forms, no app store needed | Apps needing reliable push notifications on iOS, deep device access, or store presence |

The saving applies to the app, not the whole project. The backend, admin portal and integrations cost the same whichever front-end approach you pick. ## A worked example: field service app for 60 technicians

**Here is an illustrative estimate for a common business app, built component by component.** The brief: technicians receive jobs on their phones, work offline on site, capture photos and signatures, and sync results back to the company's job management system. Office staff manage jobs in a web portal. Staff sign in with their existing Microsoft accounts.

| Component | Estimate (AUD, ex GST) |
|---|---|
| Discovery, workflows and UX design | $14,000 |
| Flutter app for iOS and Android, about 12 screens, offline job queue, camera and signature capture | $48,000 |
| Backend API, database and file storage in an Australian cloud region | $22,000 |
| Office web portal for dispatch and reporting | $12,000 |
| Two-way integration with the job management system | $18,000 |
| Single sign-on with the company's identity provider | $6,000 |
| Device testing, beta rollout, store or private distribution | $9,000 |
| **Total** | **$129,000** |

**The arithmetic for a native alternative.** If the $48,000 Flutter app represents a 35% saving on two native apps, the native equivalent is $48,000 ÷ 0.65 = about $73,800. The project total rises to roughly $129,000 − $48,000 + $73,800 = $154,800, an increase of about $25,800. Every future feature also needs building twice.

**Including GST:** $129,000 × 1.1 = $141,900.

**First-year ongoing costs:** maintenance at 15% of the build is $19,350; hosting at $200 a month is $2,400; the Apple Developer Program is US$99 a year and Google Play a one-off US$25 at the time of writing. Call it about $22,000 a year before new features.

## What app costs are usually left out of the quote?

**Quotes usually cover design, build and launch, and leave out store fees, running costs and the yearly platform update cycle.** Check for these:

- **GST** at 10% on top of ex GST quotes.
- **Developer accounts.** At the time of writing, Apple charges US$99 per membership year, billed in local currency, and Google Play a one-time US$25 registration fee.
- **Store commissions.** Apple takes 15% of paid app and in-app purchase revenue under its Small Business Program (for developers with up to US$1 million in proceeds) and a higher standard rate above that. Most B2B apps avoid this because customers pay by invoice or on the web, but check the store rules for your model.
- **Hosting and backend services.** AppGurus puts hosting at $50 to $500+ a month depending on traffic. Push notifications, maps, SMS and analytics may add per-use charges.
- **Private distribution.** Staff apps are often distributed through Apple Business Manager or managed Google Play with a device management tool, which has its own licence cost.
- **Annual OS updates.** iOS and Android ship major versions every year. Budget for testing and fixes each time.
- **Security testing** for apps handling personal, health or payment data.

## How can you lower the cost of an app?

**Pick the cheapest platform approach that meets the need, and resist building features the first users won't touch.**

1. **Question whether it needs to be a native app at all.** If users sit at desks or use company devices, a web app may do the job.
2. **Use one cross-platform codebase** unless a specific requirement rules it out.
3. **Reuse your existing identity provider** so users sign in with accounts they already have, rather than building registration and password reset.
4. **Build the admin portal lean.** Staff tools can use a standard component library; they don't need consumer polish.
5. **Get integration access early.** Test credentials for your CRM or ERP in week one save paid waiting time later.
6. **Stage the launch.** Release to one team or region, learn, then extend.
