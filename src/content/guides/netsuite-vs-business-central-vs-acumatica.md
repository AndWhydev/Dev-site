## Have you genuinely outgrown Xero or MYOB?

**You have outgrown your accounting software when the workarounds around it cost more each month than an ERP would, not when it merely feels small.** Xero and MYOB Business, with good add ons, run many businesses well past 50 staff. An ERP is a multi year commitment with an implementation project attached, so be sure first.

Count how many of these are true:

- You run several legal entities and consolidate them in spreadsheets.
- Stock sits in more than one warehouse, and you assemble or manufacture.
- Purchase approvals, credit limits or job budgets need enforcing, not just reporting.
- Month end takes days because data arrives from four or five add on apps.
- Auditors or a lender ask for controls and trails the current setup cannot show.
- Your inventory or job system and your ledger disagree every month.

One or two is a reason to fix integrations. Three or more is a reason to shortlist an ERP. If inventory is the main pain, compare a dedicated inventory platform before a full ERP.

## Who are the three contenders, and who usually picks each?

**Business Central is the Microsoft option, NetSuite is the cloud suite for growth and multiple entities, and MYOB Acumatica is the local option with payroll built in.** Prices and features below are from vendor pages at the time of writing (8 November 2026).

| | Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central | Oracle NetSuite | MYOB Acumatica |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typical buyer | Microsoft 365 businesses, distribution, light manufacturing, services | Fast growing and multi entity groups, ecommerce, software and services | Australian and NZ mid size businesses wanting local payroll and support |
| Licensing | Published, per user per month | Quote based contract; modules licensed separately | Acumatica prices by applications, usage and deployment rather than seats; MYOB quotes locally |
| Australian payroll | Not in the Australian localisation | SuitePeople Payroll covers US employees | Built in, with STP and SuperStream |
| Customisation | AL extensions | SuiteScript and the SuiteCloud tools | Low code tools and customisation framework |

## How do the licensing models compare in AUD?

**Only Business Central publishes Australian prices; the other two are quoted.** That does not make Business Central cheaper, but it makes it easier to model.

| Business Central licence (at the time of writing) | Price, paid yearly, ex GST | Who needs it |
|---|---|---|
| Essentials | AU$119.70 a user a month | Finance, sales, purchasing and inventory users |
| Premium | AU$164.60 a user a month | Adds service management and manufacturing |
| Team Members | AU$12.00 a user a month | Light users who read data, approve and update selected records |

A worked example: 15 Essentials users and 30 Team Members cost (15 × $119.70 + 30 × $12) × 12 = $25,866 a year in licences, before partner fees, add ons or payroll.

NetSuite says its modules can be licensed at any point in the contract and that fees vary by module. MYOB's own comparison page warns that NetSuite costs can rise as introductory discounts expire or modules are added; treat that as a competitor's claim, but do ask every vendor for renewal terms in writing. Acumatica's global pricing page says it charges for the applications you implement, your expected usage and resources, and your deployment choice, not per user seat. That model favours businesses with many occasional users.

## What about Australian payroll and Single Touch Payroll?

**Payroll is the sharpest difference between the three, and it matters more now that Payday Super applies from 1 July 2026.** The ATO's Payday Super pages describe how employers must calculate, pay and report super guarantee on payday from that date.

- **Business Central:** Microsoft's Australia local functionality page lists GST, withholding tax for suppliers without an ABN, BAS business units, ABN and adjustment notes, Peppol PINT A-NZ e-invoicing, EFT and payment times reporting. It lists no payroll, and taxable payments annual reporting is planned for 2027 release wave 1. Expect a separate payroll system or a partner add on.
- **NetSuite:** SuitePeople Payroll is described as a full service payroll solution for employees in the United States. Australian payroll runs through a partner product or a separate system.
- **MYOB Acumatica:** MYOB lists Single Touch Payroll, SuperStream, leave calculations, public holiday processing and award rate updates within MYOB Acumatica Payroll.

A separate payroll system is not a problem in itself, and many businesses prefer a specialist one. It does mean one more integration for employee costs, leave liabilities and job costing.

## What drives implementation cost and overruns?

**Partner implementation is usually the largest cost in year one, and scope drift is the usual cause of overruns.** All three are sold and implemented mainly through partners, so you are choosing a partner as much as a product. We do not publish implementation ranges here because they depend so heavily on scope and partner; get written estimates from at least two partners for the same scope.

Common reasons ERP projects run over:

1. **Data migration** left until late, with years of inconsistent product and customer records.
2. **Customisation** chosen instead of changing a process, then repeated at every upgrade.
3. **Integrations** to ecommerce, CRM, warehouse and payroll scoped as "connectors" and discovered to need real logic.
4. **No internal owner** with the time and authority to make decisions quickly.
5. **Reporting** requirements captured after go live instead of before design.

## How do customisation approaches differ?

**Business Central uses AL extensions, NetSuite uses SuiteScript and the SuiteCloud tools, and MYOB Acumatica offers low code tools and a customisation framework.** Microsoft describes extensions as a model where you add functionality to existing objects rather than editing the base application, which keeps upgrades safer. NetSuite describes SuiteScript, the SuiteCloud Development Framework and a SuiteAgent framework for custom agents. MYOB points to low code workflow, dashboard and report tools.

Whichever you choose, the rule is the same: customise only where your process is a genuine advantage, keep the change in a separate extension or project, and test it at every vendor update.

## How do CRM and ecommerce integrations usually work?

**Decide which system owns each record before you pick a connector.** A typical pattern: the CRM owns leads and opportunities; the ERP owns customers once they trade, products, prices, stock and invoices; the ecommerce platform owns the storefront and pushes orders to the ERP. NetSuite also offers its own CRM, which removes one integration if your sales team is happy to use it.

Where marketplace connectors cannot express your rules, a small integration service in your own cloud account is usually cheaper and more reliable than heavy customisation inside the ERP. ## What should you ask in every ERP demo?

**Make vendors and partners demonstrate your hardest processes with your own data, not their standard script.** A polished demo of a standard sales order tells you little. Send each shortlisted partner the same pack before the demo: a sample of real products, customers and transactions, and three scenarios that currently cause the most pain.

Questions worth asking each of them:

- Show a month end close for our entities, including intercompany and consolidation.
- Show how a pay run's costs reach job costing and the general ledger, including the payroll system you propose.
- Show what changes, and who fixes it, when the vendor releases a major update.
- Which of our requirements need customisation, and what will that cost to maintain each year?
- Who from your team will work on our project, and how many similar Australian implementations have they led?

Score the answers in writing straight after each demo. The partner who says "we would not customise that, change the process" is often the safer choice.
