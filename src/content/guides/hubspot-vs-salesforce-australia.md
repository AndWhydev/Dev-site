## HubSpot or Salesforce: which fits an Australian business?

**Pick HubSpot if you want one system for marketing and sales that your own team can run. Pick Salesforce if your sales process is complex enough to justify an administrator, and you want a platform you can shape almost without limit.** Both are mature, both work well in Australia, and both get expensive when chosen for the wrong reasons.

This comparison is written for businesses of roughly 10 to 200 staff. Prices are from each vendor's pricing pages at the time of writing (8 November 2026). HubSpot shows prices in US dollars; Salesforce's Australian page shows Australian dollars.

| Question | HubSpot | Salesforce |
|---|---|---|
| Built for | Marketing led growth, inbound leads, teams without a CRM admin | Complex B2B sales, many teams, deep reporting and approvals |
| Time to useful | Weeks | Weeks to months, depending on configuration |
| Who runs it day to day | A capable marketing or sales operations person | An administrator, often with a partner |
| Mid tier list price | Sales Hub Professional US$90 a seat a month, billed annually | Pro Suite AU$140 a user a month, billed annually |
| Australian hosting | Australian data centre listed | Region options via Hyperforce; confirm for your org |
| AI agents | Agent Hub and Breeze, paid with bundled HubSpot Credits | Agentforce, paid with Flex Credits, conversations or user licences |
| Customisation ceiling | Custom objects and apps on higher tiers | Very high: custom objects, code, platform apps |

## Who is each CRM built for?

**HubSpot is built for businesses that win customers through content, email and inbound enquiries and want sales to see the whole marketing history.** Its strength is that the CRM, forms, email, landing pages and service inbox share one database with little setup.

**Salesforce is built for organisations whose sales motion has many steps, products, territories or approvals.** Its strength is the platform underneath: almost any object, rule or screen can be modelled, and a large partner ecosystem exists to do it.

A useful test is who will own the CRM in a year. If the answer is "our marketing manager, a few hours a week", HubSpot is the safer choice. If it is "a dedicated administrator", Salesforce becomes realistic.

## What does each cost at 5, 25 and 100 seats?

**At list prices, the mid tiers of both CRMs land in a similar range per seat. The totals diverge on onboarding, upgrades and admin time.** The table below shows first year licence costs only, billed annually. HubSpot figures are converted at the Reserve Bank's rate of US$0.69 to the Australian dollar on 6 November 2026.

| Plan (at the time of writing) | 5 seats, year one | 25 seats, year one | 100 seats, year one |
|---|---|---|---|
| HubSpot Sales Hub Professional: US$90 × seats × 12 + US$1,500 onboarding | US$6,900 (about $10,000 AUD) | US$28,500 (about $41,300 AUD) | US$109,500 (about $158,700 AUD) |
| HubSpot Sales Hub Enterprise: US$150 × seats × 12 + US$3,500 onboarding | US$12,500 (about $18,100 AUD) | US$48,500 (about $70,300 AUD) | US$183,500 (about $265,900 AUD) |
| Salesforce Pro Suite: AU$140 × seats × 12 | AU$8,400 | AU$42,000 | AU$168,000 |
| Salesforce Core: AU$273 × seats × 12 | AU$16,380 | AU$81,900 | AU$327,600 |

Three things the arithmetic leaves out:

1. **Required onboarding.** HubSpot's pricing page states the Professional and Enterprise onboarding fees are required and one time. Salesforce implementations are usually done by a partner and quoted separately.
2. **Other hubs and add ons.** Marketing contacts, service seats, extra credits and premium support all change the HubSpot total. On Salesforce, features such as Agentforce, Tableau and planning tools are listed as available for purchase on lower editions.
3. **People.** A Salesforce administrator, in house or through a partner retainer, can cost more each year than the licences for a mid size team.

HubSpot's Starter tier starts at US$7 a seat a month on its page at the time of writing, with discounts marked for new customers only, and Salesforce lists Starter Suite at AU$35. Both are fine for a small team with a simple pipeline.

## Where does your CRM data live?

**HubSpot can host your account in Australia. With Salesforce, ask which Hyperforce region your org will run in and get it in writing.** HubSpot's data hosting FAQ says its product infrastructure runs on AWS in the United States, Canada, Australia and the European Union (Germany), that customer data is stored in the assigned location, and that accounts are assigned by the location of the sign up IP address. Paid accounts can change data centre.

Salesforce's Hyperforce page describes choosing regions for where Salesforce applications run, naming the US, UK, Germany, India, Japan "and more". Availability depends on the products in your org, so confirm the Australian position with your account executive before relying on it.

Hosting location is not the whole story. HubSpot itself notes that marketplace apps, partners, sub processors, travelling staff and its own support teams may process data outside the hosting location. Under APP 8 of the Privacy Act, sending personal information overseas is allowed, but you must take reasonable steps so the recipient handles it consistently with the Australian Privacy Principles, and you generally remain accountable. Our [data residency explainer](/guides/data-residency-vs-data-sovereignty) covers what onshore hosting does and does not protect against.

## Who runs it after go live?

**HubSpot can usually be run part time by a capable operations person. Salesforce usually needs a named administrator once you move past the defaults.** That difference matters more to total cost than any line on the pricing page.

Signs you are underinvesting in admin, on either platform:

- Fields multiply, and nobody knows which ones reports use.
- Automations fire twice or not at all after someone edits a property.
- Sales staff keep their own spreadsheet because the CRM view is wrong.
- Every new report request becomes an export to Excel.

## What about Xero, MYOB, telephony and SMS?

**Both CRMs connect to Australian accounting, phone and SMS tools through marketplace apps, and both need careful checking before you trust the sync.** For Xero or MYOB, ask what objects move (contacts, products, quotes, invoices, payments), which way, and how often. One way invoice creation is common; reliable two way payment status is not.

For telephony and SMS, check that the provider supports Australian numbers and branded sender IDs. From 1 July 2026, businesses sending texts under a branded sender ID must register it with ACMA, as our guide to [sending business SMS in Australia](/guides/business-sms-rules-australia) explains. ## How do Breeze and Agentforce compare in practice?

**Both vendors now sell AI agents that draft emails, research prospects and handle routine service questions, and both meter them with credits.** The difference is packaging.

- **HubSpot** bundles HubSpot Credits with each Sales Hub plan: 500 on Starter, 3,000 on Professional and 5,000 on Enterprise at the time of writing. Agents such as the prospecting agent and the Agent Builder draw on those credits.
- **Salesforce** sells Agentforce on consumption: Flex Credits at AU$700 per 100,000, or AU$2.80 per conversation, plus an Agentforce user licence at AU$7 a user a month that requires Flex Credits. The Max edition includes 2.75 million Flex Credits per org per year.

Before you budget, run one agent on a real workflow for a month and measure credit use. Agents that touch customers also need review rules and a person accountable for their output. If the agent must work across several systems, a purpose built agent may be cheaper than metered CRM credits; see [AI agent development](/services/ai-agent-development).

## How painful is switching later?

**Records move in days; automations, reports and integrations take weeks to rebuild.** Neither platform's workflows, sequences or report definitions export to the other. Plan a switch as a project:

1. Export every object, including activity history, owners and consent records.
2. Map fields and decide what not to bring.
3. Rebuild automations and reports, then test with real users.
4. Reconnect integrations and run both systems briefly in parallel.

## When does neither fit, and custom makes sense?

**Neither fits when the CRM is really running your operations rather than your pipeline.** Jobs, bookings, case files, compliance records and customer portals can be forced into either platform, but the cost of custom objects, higher tiers and workarounds adds up. Our [custom CRM cost guide](/guides/custom-crm-development-cost-australia) sets out when a build pays for itself, and the [build vs buy guide](/guides/build-vs-buy-software) covers the wider decision. For many businesses the best answer is to keep HubSpot or Salesforce for sales and build the operations piece alongside it.
