## What does an AI receptionist cost? The three ways to pay

**You can rent an AI receptionist on a monthly plan, build a custom voice agent on a voice platform, or keep or hire a person, and each has a different cost shape.** Plans are cheap to start and charge more as minutes grow. Custom builds cost more upfront and less per minute. People cost the most but do the most.

| Option | Upfront (AUD) | Ongoing | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Subscription AI receptionist | Usually little or nothing; some charge setup | About $150 to $400 a month for most small businesses, plus overage of about 27 to 45 cents a minute | Low to moderate call volumes, standard booking and message taking |
| Custom AI voice agent | About $5,000 to $10,000 for a simple booking agent; $15,000 to $40,000 for a focused agent build | Roughly 15 to 20 cents a minute in platform and phone costs, plus hosting, monitoring and maintenance | Higher volumes, your own systems and rules, specific handover |
| Human receptionist (full time) | Recruitment and training | At least about $58,500 a year at the National Minimum Wage with super; more under most awards and with on costs | Judgement, complaints, in person and office work |

Subscription figures come from Binary Elements' November 2026 Australian guide, which lists its own plans at $149 a month (200 minutes, then 45 cents a minute) and $299 a month (600 minutes, then 40 cents a minute), and reports Siptalk at 27.5 cents a minute with a typical spend of about $400 a month. Custom build figures are from Flowtivity (an AI receptionist or appointment booking agent at $5,000 to $10,000) and Sand Labs (a focused agent build at $15,000 to $40,000). Neither build range is specific to voice, and voice usually sits at the upper end because of the telephony and speech work. These are published market figures, not our prices.

## How much does a subscription AI receptionist cost?

**Most plans bundle a set number of minutes for a flat monthly fee, then charge per minute.** That makes the headline price misleading: what you actually pay depends on how long your calls are and how many you get.

Before comparing plans, work out your minutes. A simple estimate:

> calls a month × average call length in minutes = minutes a month

For example, 10 calls a day, 22 working days, at 3 minutes each, is 660 minutes a month. If you also want after hours cover, add those calls.

| Monthly minutes | Plan with 200 minutes at $149, then 45c | Plan with 600 minutes at $299, then 40c | Pure per minute at 27.5c |
|---|---|---|---|
| 200 | $149 | $299 | $55 |
| 600 | $149 + (400 × $0.45) = $329 | $299 | $165 |
| 1,500 | $149 + (1,300 × $0.45) = $734 | $299 + (900 × $0.40) = $659 | $413 |
| 3,000 | $149 + (2,800 × $0.45) = $1,409 | $299 + (2,400 × $0.40) = $1,259 | $825 |

These use the published rates above as examples only; plans differ in what's included (integrations, CRM, SMS, number porting), and you should check whether prices include GST. Pure per minute pricing usually has a minimum spend or extra fees, so it isn't always as cheap as the column suggests.

## How much does it cost per minute to run a custom voice agent?

**On today's voice platforms the running cost of a custom agent is roughly 15 to 20 Australian cents a minute for a typical setup, and it can be lower or much higher depending on the model and voice.** Here's the arithmetic, using listed prices at the time of writing.

Retell AI, a voice agent platform, lists pay as you go pricing of US$0.07 to US$0.31 a minute. Its own example configuration comes to US$0.11 a minute: US$0.04 for the language model, US$0.055 for voice infrastructure and US$0.015 for text to speech, with telephony billed separately when you bring your own carrier. Premium voices cost more: ElevenLabs voices are listed at US$0.04 to US$0.10 a minute, and speech to speech models at up to US$0.38 a minute.

Twilio lists receiving calls on an Australian local number at US$0.01 a minute plus US$3.00 a month for the number, and on a mobile number at US$0.05 a minute plus US$20.00 a month.

**Worked example: 600 minutes a month on a local number**

- AI platform: 600 × US$0.11 = US$66.00
- Phone line: 600 × US$0.01 = US$6.00, plus US$3.00 for the number
- Total: US$75.00 a month
- At the Reserve Bank's 6 November 2026 rate of US$0.69 per A$1: US$75 ÷ 0.69 ≈ **A$109 a month**, or about 18 cents a minute

**The same setup at 3,000 minutes a month**

- AI platform: 3,000 × US$0.11 = US$330
- Phone line: 3,000 × US$0.01 = US$30, plus US$3
- Total: US$363 ≈ **A$526 a month**

On top of that, budget for hosting any integration code, monitoring, recording storage if you keep audio, and maintenance. A common planning figure for software maintenance is 15% to 20% of the build a year; on a $25,000 build that's $3,750 to $5,000 a year, or about $310 to $420 a month. ## Subscription or custom: where is the crossover?

**At a few hundred minutes a month a subscription is cheaper; at a few thousand, a custom agent can be, once you count the build over three years.** This illustration uses the figures above and stated assumptions. It isn't a quote.

**Assumptions:** a $25,000 custom build (inside the $15,000 to $40,000 focused agent range), maintenance at 15% a year ($3,750), and the running costs worked out above.

| Monthly minutes | Subscription ($299 plan, 40c overage), 3 years | Custom agent, 3 years | Cheaper on cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 600 | $299 × 36 = $10,764 | $25,000 + (3 × $3,750) + ($109 × 36) ≈ $40,200 | Subscription, by a long way |
| 3,000 | $1,259 × 36 = $45,324 | $25,000 + (3 × $3,750) + ($526 × 36) ≈ $55,190 | Subscription, narrowly |
| 6,000 | ($299 + 5,400 × $0.40) × 36 = $88,524 | $25,000 + (3 × $3,750) + ($1,048 × 36) ≈ $73,980 | Custom |

The 6,000 minute running cost is (6,000 × US$0.11) + (6,000 × US$0.01) + US$3 = US$723, or about A$1,048 a month.

The honest conclusion: on cost alone, most small businesses should start on a subscription. A custom build is worth it when cost isn't the only issue, for example when the agent must book into a system the subscriptions don't support, follow triage rules specific to your business, run several call flows, keep recordings and transcripts in infrastructure you control, or hand over to staff in a particular way.

## How does it compare with a human receptionist?

**A person costs many times more than an AI receptionist, but does a different job.** Use award and minimum wage rates rather than vendor claims when you compare.

The floor, using official figures:

- National Minimum Wage from 1 July 2026: $26.44 an hour, or $1,004.90 for a 38 hour week (Fair Work Ombudsman)
- Annual wage: $1,004.90 × 52 = $52,254.80
- Super guarantee at 12% (ATO): $6,270.58
- **Minimum annual cost: about $58,525**, before leave loading, workers compensation, payroll tax where it applies, equipment, and any award rate above the minimum

Binary Elements estimates a full time receptionist at roughly $60,000 to $75,000 a year once super, leave and on costs are included, and a 20 hour a week part timer at about half that. And a full time person covers about 38 hours a week, not evenings, weekends or lunch.

What a person does that an AI receptionist shouldn't:

- Handle complaints, distress and anything that needs empathy or authority
- Make judgement calls on unusual requests and VIP customers
- Do the rest of the job: front desk, payments, admin, stock, follow ups

That's why most businesses use AI for after hours, overflow and the routine calls, and keep people for the rest. The comparison that matters is usually AI receptionist versus voicemail and missed calls, not AI versus a staff member.

## What are the hidden costs?

**The monthly fee is rarely the whole bill.** Plan for these, whichever option you choose:

| Hidden cost | What it involves |
|---|---|
| Phone system changes | Forwarding rules, porting a number, or connecting your existing phone system; Binary Elements lists phone system integration from $79 a month on its plans |
| Integrations | Calendars, job management, CRM or practice software; often quoted separately on subscriptions, and the main build cost in custom work |
| Overage | Minutes above your plan, especially in busy seasons |
| GST | 10% on top of prices quoted ex GST |
| Call recording and privacy | Recording disclosures, retention settings, and checking where recordings and transcripts are stored |
| Testing and tuning | Time to test with real callers, Australian accents, local place names and noisy lines before going live |
| Ongoing review | Someone checking a sample of calls each week and updating answers, prices and rules |
| Handover failures | The cost of a caller who wanted a person and couldn't reach one |
