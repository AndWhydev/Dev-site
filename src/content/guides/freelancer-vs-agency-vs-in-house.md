## Freelancer vs agency vs in-house developers: which is right for you?

**The right model depends on how long the work lasts, how central software is to your business, and how much management time you have.** None of the three is best in general. Each is cheapest in a particular situation and expensive outside it.

| | Freelancer or contractor | Agency | In-house employee |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typical Sydney cost (AUD, ex GST) | Freelancers $80 to $150/hr; senior contractors $800 to $1,100/day | $120 to $250/hr blended, larger firms higher | About $120 to $135 per productive hour for a senior engineer, fully loaded |
| Time to start | Days to weeks | Two to six weeks | Often three months or more |
| Skills you get | One person's skills | A team: engineering, design, QA, DevOps, project management | What you hire, one person at a time |
| Management load on you | High: you direct the work | Low to medium: they manage delivery | High: you manage people and careers |
| Continuity risk | High: one person, may move on | Medium: depends on handover and documentation | Low while they stay; high when a sole engineer resigns |
| Best for | Defined tasks, specialist skills, extra capacity | Projects with a start and end, building v1 | Core product, continuous change, long horizon |
| Code ownership by default | Contractor owns it unless assigned in writing | Agency owns it unless assigned in writing | Employer owns work done in the course of employment |

The rate figures come from Robert Walters' 2026 salary guide, Re:Sourced's 2026 contractor rate guide (Sydney senior engineers $800 to $1,100 a day), Software Co's Sydney figures for freelancers, and Conduct's 2026 agency ranges. ## What does an in-house developer really cost?

**A senior developer on a $165,000 salary costs an employer roughly $203,000 a year, which works out at about $120 to $125 per productive hour.** Salary is only the starting point. Here is the arithmetic for a Sydney senior backend engineer at the midpoint of Robert Walters' NSW range ($150,000 to $180,000).

| Cost item | Basis | Annual cost (AUD) |
|---|---|---|
| Base salary | Robert Walters NSW senior backend midpoint | $165,000 |
| Superannuation | 12% super guarantee (from 1 July 2025) | $19,800 |
| Payroll tax | 5.45% of wages including super, only if your NSW payroll exceeds the $1.2 million threshold | $10,072 |
| Workers compensation | icare NSW 2026 to 2027 rate for computer consultancy, 0.218% of wages | $360 |
| Equipment, software, training | Assumption: laptop, licences, courses, conference | $8,000 |
| **Total employer cost** | | **$203,232** |

Now the hours. A full-time year is 52 weeks of 38 hours. Take out 4 weeks of annual leave, 10 days of personal and carer's leave (the National Employment Standards entitlement), and about 10 public holidays, and you have roughly 44 working weeks, or 1,672 hours.

$203,232 ÷ 1,672 hours = **about $122 per productive hour**, before recruitment fees, management time, office space and the months it takes to hire. It's also before the ramp-up period: a new engineer is rarely fully productive in the first two to three months.

If your business sits under the payroll tax threshold, the figure drops to around $115. If you need a lead or architect (Robert Walters puts NSW solution architects at $195,000 to $220,000), it rises well above $140.

## When is hiring in-house the better choice?

**Keep development in-house (rather than outsourcing it) when software is a permanent part of how you compete and there's enough continuous work to keep at least two engineers busy for years.** An employee builds knowledge of your business that no outside party can match, and per productive hour they're usually cheaper than an agency.

In-house makes sense if:

- You're building a software product, or software is central to operations, with a multi-year roadmap.
- You can hire at least two engineers, or one plus an external partner, so knowledge doesn't live in one head.
- You have someone who can lead engineering: set standards, review code and hire well.
- You're prepared for the recruitment timeline and the cost of a bad hire.

The common failure mode is the sole in-house developer. One person carries every system in their head, has nobody to review their code, and when they resign the business is exposed. If you can only justify one hire, pair them with an agency or senior contractor for review and cover.

## When is a freelancer or contractor the better choice?

**Use a freelancer or contractor when you know exactly what needs doing and can direct the work yourself.** You pay only for the hours used, you can get specialist skills quickly, and there's no long-term commitment.

Good fits:

1. Adding capacity to an existing team that already has technical leadership.
2. A specialist job: a performance fix, a security review, a specific integration.
3. A small, well-specified build, such as a Shopify customisation or a single integration.
4. Covering parental leave or a gap while you hire.

Where it goes wrong: a single freelancer building a whole business system with nobody technical on your side checking the work. You carry the project management, the design decisions, the testing and the continuity risk. Also watch the legal edges. Since 26 August 2024, whether someone is a contractor or employee is decided by the whole of the relationship in practice, so a long-running "contractor" working fixed hours under your direction may legally be an employee.

## When is an agency the better choice?

**Use an agency when you need a whole team for a defined project, fast, without building an engineering function.** You get engineers, design, testing, DevOps and project management together, and the agency absorbs the cost of people between projects, sick leave and turnover.

Agencies fit best when:

- You're building a first version and don't yet know whether you'll need a permanent team.
- The project needs several skills at once for three to twelve months.
- Nobody in-house can lead engineering or judge technical quality.
- You want a fixed price or a clear budget rather than an open-ended headcount cost.

The honest downsides: the hourly rate is higher, you're one client among several, and a poor agency can leave you with code nobody else understands. That last risk is manageable. Insist on code in your own repository from day one, documentation, and written IP assignment. ## Who owns the code under each model?

**Employees' work belongs to the employer automatically; contractors' and agencies' work does not.** Under section 35(6) of the Copyright Act 1968, copyright in a work made by an employee under a contract of employment belongs to the employer. There's no equivalent rule for contractors or agencies: the author owns copyright unless it's assigned, and section 196(3) says an assignment has no effect unless it's in writing and signed by or on behalf of the assignor.

Moral rights are separate. Under the Act they can't be assigned at all, so good contracts include the author's written consent to the kinds of changes you'll make to the code. Our [contract checklist](/guides/software-development-contract-checklist) covers IP assignment, moral rights consents and source code access in more detail.

## Should you hire a developer or use an agency? A decision guide

**Start from the length and shape of the work, then check who on your side can lead it.** If nobody internally can judge technical quality, rule out a lone freelancer first, whatever the rate. If the work will never really stop, rule out a pure project engagement. What remains is usually obvious.

| Your situation | Best fit |
|---|---|
| Continuous product development for 3+ years, can hire 2+ engineers | In-house, possibly with an agency for peaks |
| One-off project, 3 to 12 months, no engineering lead | Agency |
| Existing tech team needs an extra pair of hands or a specialist | Contractor or staff augmentation |
| Small, well-specified task, you can check the work | Freelancer |
| First version of a new product, future team size unknown | Agency to build, then hire and hand over |
| Only one developer affordable, system is business-critical | One hire plus an external partner for review and cover |
