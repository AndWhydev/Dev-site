## Eight questions to ask any developer

Ask every firm the same questions, in writing, and compare the answers side by side.

1. **Who will write the code, and where are they based?** Ask for the people on your project, not the company headcount, and whether any work is subcontracted.
2. **How do you scope and price the work?** Find out whether there is a scoping or discovery step, whether it is paid, and what document you receive at the end.
3. **Is the price fixed or time and materials, and what happens when the scope changes?** Ask to see how a change request is quoted and approved.
4. **Who owns the code and the intellectual property, and when?** Check whether ownership passes on payment, at the end, or never, and whether you get access to the code repository during the build.
5. **How often will I see working software?** Regular demos or a test version you can use yourself show progress better than status reports.
6. **What happens after launch?** Ask about the warranty period, ongoing support, hosting, and what maintenance costs each year.
7. **Can I speak to a past client with a similar project?** A short call with a reference tells you how the firm handled problems, not just how the project looked at the end.
8. **Where will my data be stored, and how do you handle privacy and security?** If your software holds personal information, the [Australian Privacy Principles](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles) apply to how it is handled, so ask how the firm designs for them.

## What the table tells you

The firms differ most on two questions from the list above: where the engineers sit, and whether scoping is a free conversation or a paid piece of work with a written output. Neither approach is right for everyone. A free consultation costs you nothing but usually ends in an estimate; a paid scoping step costs money up front but should give you a document you can take to any developer.

Use the table as a starting point, then ask each firm you shortlist the eight questions directly. For the broader decision, see [how to choose a software development company](/guides/how-to-choose-a-software-development-company).
