## Which paper forms should you digitise first, and which should you delete?

**Start with forms that are filled in often, read by someone who acts on them, and painful when they go missing.** Delete the rest. Digitising a form nobody reads just moves the waste to a screen.

Gather one copy of every form in use, from the site office, the vans and the back of the warehouse. For each one, answer four questions:

1. **Who fills it in, and how often?**
2. **Who reads it, and what do they do with it?** If the honest answer is "it goes in a folder", ask whether a law, contract or client actually requires it.
3. **What happens if it is late, wrong or lost?** Rework, a failed audit, a payroll error, an unpaid invoice.
4. **Does it duplicate another form?** Timesheets that repeat the job card, or two prestart checklists with the same questions.

Then rank them. A typical first wave for a field business:

| Form | Why it goes first |
|---|---|
| Timesheets and job cards | Feed payroll and invoicing; errors cost money every week |
| Prestart and vehicle checklists | Daily, simple, and evidence of safety duties |
| Site inspections and quality checks | Need photos and sign off; clients ask for them |
| Incident and hazard reports | Time sensitive; must reach the right person immediately |
| Delivery and completion sign offs | Trigger invoicing and settle disputes |

## Form builder or custom app?

**A form builder is the right start for most businesses. Build custom when submissions must drive jobs, pricing or compliance logic in other systems, or when the form is your product.**

| Need | Microsoft Forms, Jotform | SafetyCulture, GoCanvas | Custom app |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple office forms and surveys | Best fit | Overkill | Overkill |
| Offline field inspections with photos and signatures | Limited | Best fit | Fits |
| Scoring, follow up actions and reporting on inspections | Limited | Strong | Fits |
| Logic that depends on the job, asset or customer record in your system | Weak | Some, via integrations | Best fit |
| Submissions that create jobs, quotes or invoices automatically | Via automation tools | Via integrations | Best fit |
| Forms delivered to your own clients under your brand | Limited | Limited | Best fit |
| Cost model | Included in Microsoft 365 or low monthly fee | Per seat per month | Build cost plus hosting |

At the time of writing, SafetyCulture lists a free plan for teams of up to 10 and Premium at $24 per seat per month billed annually, excluding taxes, and GoCanvas prices per user per month with a three user minimum. For a crew of 30 on Premium with annual billing, that is about $720 a month, which is usually far cheaper than a build. A custom app makes sense when the integration and logic are the value, not the form itself.

## How do you design forms for the field?

**Design for the worst conditions: no reception, gloves, glare, noise and a worker who wants to get back to the job.** A form that works in the office can fail completely on site.

- **Offline first.** The app must open, save and queue submissions with no signal, then sync later without losing anything. Show clearly what has and hasn't synced.
- **Big targets, high contrast.** Large buttons, short labels, dark text on light backgrounds that stay readable in sun.
- **Choices over typing.** Pass, fail and not applicable buttons, picklists, photo capture and voice notes instead of free text.
- **Prefill everything known.** Site, job number, asset, worker and date should come from the job, not be typed.
- **Short forms.** Split a 60 question audit into sections, and hide questions that don't apply based on earlier answers.
- **Required only where it matters.** Make a field mandatory only if a missing answer genuinely blocks the next step.

## How do photos, GPS, timestamps and signatures become evidence?

**Evidence is only useful if it is captured at the moment, tied to the record and protected from later changes.** Paper rarely meets that bar; a well built form can.

Capture these automatically with each submission:

- **Timestamps** for when the form was started, each section completed and submitted, from the device and the server.
- **Location** at submission, where the worker has agreed to location capture.
- **Photos taken in the app**, not uploaded from the camera roll, with time and location attached, and annotations stored separately from the original image.
- **Signatures** from the worker and, where needed, the client or supervisor, with their name and role.
- **An unchangeable record.** Once submitted, edits create a new version with who changed what and why.

The Attorney-General's Department explains that under the Electronic Transactions Act, records can be stored electronically if they can be reopened and read again, and the method reliably prevents them being altered. A submission store with version history and no silent edits meets that intent.

## How do submissions turn into actions?

**Every form should end in something happening: a job created, a person alerted, a report sent or a record updated.** If nothing happens, the form is still paper, just on a screen.

Typical rules:

- A failed checklist item creates a defect job assigned to the right trade, with the photo attached.
- An incident report alerts the supervisor and safety lead immediately, with location and photos.
- A completed job sign off triggers the invoice in Xero or MYOB.
- Timesheets feed payroll and job costing without retyping.
- Weekly, managers receive a summary of open defects, overdue inspections and trends by site.

Incident reporting deserves special care. Safe Work Australia notes that a business must notify its WHS regulator immediately after becoming aware of certain serious incidents, and that model WHS Act amendments published in December 2025 extend notification duties to more incident types once each jurisdiction adopts them. Your form should help the person decide whether an incident is notifiable and route it straight away; your regulator's guidance decides the rules. In NSW, SafeWork NSW also points businesses to the register of injuries.

## How do you digitise the backlog of old paper records?

**Scan in batches, make the scans searchable, and index them by the few fields people actually search on.** Then decide which originals you can destroy.

1. **Prioritise.** Start with records you are most likely to need: active sites, open warranties, assets still in service.
2. **Scan to a consistent standard**, with file names or metadata for site, date and form type.
3. **Run OCR** so typed text is searchable.
4. **Extract key fields with AI** from typed or handwritten forms, with a person checking anything the model is unsure of. Our [AI document processing service](/services/ai-document-processing) covers this.
5. **Check before destroying originals.** Some documents should stay in original form, and government bodies must follow their archives authority's rules.

## Which record keeping rules still apply?

**Going digital doesn't change how long you must keep records, only how you keep them.** Check the rules that apply to each form type.

| Record type | Rule to check | Source of the rule |
|---|---|---|
| Timesheets, job and payment records | Keep most business records for 5 years; records must be in English or easily converted | ATO record keeping rules |
| Company records | ASIC requires companies to keep records for 7 years, as the ATO notes | Corporations law |
| Incident and injury records | Notification duties and record requirements under your state's WHS laws | Your WHS regulator |
| Industry inspections and certificates | Retention set by the licensing body or contract | Licensing authority or client contract |

The ATO also asks that digital records are labelled or indexed so they can be found and extracted. Build retention into the system: each form type gets a retention period, and expired records are reviewed and deleted on schedule rather than kept forever.

## What does it cost, and how do you roll it out so staff use it?

**Indicative setup costs run from about $5,000 for a configured form builder to $100,000 for a custom offline app.** These are our estimates of typical Australian market pricing (AUD, ex GST), not quotes.

| Build | Indicative range (AUD, ex GST) | Ongoing |
|---|---|---|
| Configured form builder with integrations | $5,000 to $20,000 | Per seat subscription |
| Custom offline form app with photo and signature capture and dashboards | $40,000 to $100,000 | Hosting and maintenance |
| Backlog digitisation with OCR and indexing | $15,000 to $60,000 | Storage |

**Roll out in three steps.** Pilot with one crew for two weeks and fix every complaint. Run paper and digital side by side for a short period at the next sites.
