## How does a print business quote, schedule and invoice?

**A printer's work runs from estimate to job ticket to press and finishing schedule to dispatch and invoice, and the estimate decides whether the job makes money.** Print is quote heavy and low margin. Many jobs are small, many are repeats with changes, and every one depends on machine time that can't be in two places at once.

The flow looks the same in most shops:

1. **Enquiry and estimate.** Specification in (size, stock, colours, quantity, finishing), price out, often with several quantities.
2. **Acceptance and job ticket.** The estimate becomes a job with artwork, proof and production instructions.
3. **Prepress and proof.** Files are checked, imposed and proofed to the client.
4. **Scheduling.** The job is slotted onto a press, then guillotine, folder, binder or laminator.
5. **Production and stock.** Stock is drawn, makeready and running time recorded.
6. **Dispatch and invoice.** Delivery or courier, then an invoice to Xero or MYOB, ideally with estimate against actual reported.

A print MIS exists to hold that whole chain. The custom software question is which links your MIS can't model for your work.

## printIQ, Tharstern, PrintSmith Vision or a spreadsheet estimator?

**The established MIS products cover most commercial, digital and quick print work; the decision is about fit for your mix of work and size.** printIQ describes itself as a cloud based MIS and says it has staff in Australia, New Zealand, North America and the UK, and a printIQ news item names Banyan Software as parent of printIQ and DocketManager. Print ePS lists Tharstern for commercial, mailing and signage printers, PrintSmith Vision for small quick printers and franchises, Pace for mid sized commercial printers, Nubium as a cloud MIS for smaller commercial printers, and MarketDirect StoreFront for web to print.

| Option | Typical fit | Where it tends to strain |
|---|---|---|
| printIQ | Commercial and digital printers wanting a cloud MIS with estimating through production | Very specialised product lines with unusual pricing rules |
| Tharstern | Commercial, mailing and signage printers | Client specific storefront logic |
| PrintSmith Vision | Small quick printers and franchise networks | Complex multi site scheduling |
| Pace or Nubium | Mid sized and smaller commercial printers respectively | Local niche finishing integrations |
| Spreadsheet estimator | A specialist with one or two product types | Version control, key person risk, no link to job tickets |

## What goes into an estimate, and where do they go wrong?

**An estimate is stock plus press time plus finishing plus labour plus wastage, priced at your rates and marked up; errors usually come from imposition, makeready or wastage rules that don't match the floor.**

A worked example with illustrative figures (not market rates): 5,000 A5 flyers, printed both sides on 150gsm gloss, digital press, SRA3 sheets.

| Line | Rule | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Imposition | A5 with 3mm bleed fits 4 up on SRA3 | 1,250 sheets |
| Wastage | Allow 5% | 1,313 sheets (rounded up) |
| Stock | $0.12 per sheet | $157.56 |
| Click charge | $0.08 per side, 2 sides | $210.08 |
| Guillotine | 0.5 hours at $90 | $45.00 |
| Packing | Flat allowance | $20.00 |
| Cost before markup | | $432.64 |

Each line is a rule your MIS must hold correctly for every product. Common sources of error:

- **Imposition.** Grain direction, gripper margins and bleed change how many items fit on a sheet.
- **Makeready.** Offset presses carry setup time and setup waste that a digital press doesn't.
- **Wastage.** A flat percentage ignores that short runs waste proportionally more.
- **Finishing.** Folding, binding, laminating and die cutting have their own setup and speed curves.
- **Minimums and breaks.** Quantity breaks and minimum charges are often in the estimator's head, not the system.

A custom estimator makes sense when one product line has rules your MIS can't represent, such as large format with nesting, packaging with die lines, or a specialty process. It sits beside the MIS, takes the specification, applies your rules and pushes the priced job into the MIS by API so the job ticket and invoice stay in one place. Test it against a sample of recent jobs with known actual costs before anyone quotes with it.

## Web to print for corporate and franchise clients

**Web to print for business clients is less about the shopping cart and more about rules: who can order what, in which brand, approved by whom, charged to which cost centre.** A national franchise network might need each store to order local flyers from locked templates, with only the address and offer editable, head office approval above a set value, and monthly billing by store.

Typical storefront requirements:

- Brand locked templates with editable fields only where the client allows.
- Live proofing so the person ordering sees exactly what will print.
- Approval chains by value, product or user group.
- Cost centre or store billing, with consolidated invoicing.
- Reorder of approved items without new proofs.
- Order data flowing into the MIS as a job ticket, not an email.

Start with the storefront your MIS vendor offers, such as MarketDirect StoreFront in the Print ePS family. A custom storefront earns its place when a large client's rules, single sign on or integration with their procurement system can't be met by configuration. ## Scheduling presses and finishing

**Scheduling is the hardest part of print to automate well, because every job competes for the same few machines and finishing often becomes the bottleneck.** Most MIS products include a schedule board. Custom work here is usually narrow: feeding machine status into the schedule automatically, warning when a promised date depends on a finishing step that is already full, or showing customer service a reliable dispatch date at the moment of quoting.

## FSC chain of custody records

**If you label printed work as FSC certified or make FSC claims on sales documents, you need chain of custody certification, and your systems must track certified material through every job.** FSC says certified organisations must identify and track certified material during manufacturing and distribution, keep all records of its purchase, production and sale, and face annual audits by their certification body.

In practice that means the MIS should carry the FSC claim and certificate details from the stock purchase order, through the job ticket and any substitutions, to the invoice and delivery docket. A small custom layer can check that every job sold with an FSC claim used stock bought with one, and produce the volume summary your auditor will ask for.

## Connecting to Xero, MYOB, suppliers and equipment

**Integration is where small builds pay back quickly: invoices into accounts, supplier prices into the estimator, and job data to and from equipment.** Common links include invoices and payments syncing with Xero or MYOB, which our [Xero and MYOB integration](/services/xero-myob-integration) service covers; paper merchant price lists loaded into stock tables; courier bookings from dispatch; and job status from presses or finishing equipment where the maker offers an interface. ## Typical builds and indicative costs

**Print builds are usually focused additions to the MIS.** Expeed's 2026 survey of Australian costs puts simple internal tools at $15,000 to $40,000 and mid complexity systems at $40,000 to $150,000.

| Build | Indicative range (AUD, ex GST) | Main drivers |
|---|---|---|
| Custom estimator for a specific product line | $15k to $40k | Rule complexity, MIS API, testing against past jobs |
| Corporate or franchise web to print storefront | $30k to $80k | Templates, approval rules, single sign on, MIS integration |
| FSC claim tracking and audit reporting layer | $10k to $30k | MIS data quality, stock and supplier records |

These are planning ranges, not quotes, and exclude MIS licences and hosting. For the wider manufacturing picture see our [manufacturing](/industries/manufacturing) page.
