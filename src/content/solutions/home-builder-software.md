## How is residential building different for your systems?

**A home builder's customer is a homeowner, often spending the largest sum of their life, protected by domestic building law that controls deposits, payments, insurance and variations.** A commercial builder's systems are shaped by head contracts, subcontractor claims and security of payment. A home builder's systems are shaped by the homeowner's rights and by the questions they ask every week.

That produces a different list of requirements:

- Deposits and progress claims that stay inside state limits.
- Proof of insurance before any money is requested.
- Variations explained in plain language, with the price calculation shown.
- Selections with deadlines that match the programme.
- Progress updates the homeowner can see without phoning the supervisor.
- Warranty and defect records that last for years after handover.

Our [construction software page](/solutions/construction-software-and-ai) covers commercial work, security of payment and WHS. This page is the domestic side.

## Buildxact, Databuild, Buildertrend or Procore: what home builders use and outgrow

**Most Australian home builders run estimating and jobs in Buildxact or Databuild, and accounts in Xero or MYOB.** Buildertrend and CoConstruct are strong on client communication but were built for the US market. Simpro suits builders who also run maintenance and service work. Procore is built for larger commercial projects and is usually more than a volume or custom home builder needs. Our [trades business software](/solutions/trades-business-software) page compares the field service platforms.

Builders tend to outgrow these tools in the same places: homeowner facing views, selections, claims that follow state rules exactly, and warranty records after handover. Those are the gaps worth building for, connected to the platform you keep.

## Staged payments: what each state requires

**Each state limits deposits and controls how progress payments are structured, and the rules differ enough that claims software must be configured per state.**

| Rule | NSW (Home Building Act 1989) | Victoria (Domestic Building Contracts Act 1995) |
|---|---|---|
| Written contract | Over $5,000; small job contract $5,000 to $20,000, large job contract over $20,000 | Major domestic building contract for work over $10,000 involving more than one trade |
| Maximum deposit | 10% | 10% under $20,000; 5% at $20,000 or more |
| Progress payments | Fixed stages of a stated amount or percentage, or as work is performed with supporting invoices, or a mix | Statutory stages with set percentages by contract type |
| Cooling off | 5 clear business days for contracts over $20,000 | Set out in the contract and CAV guidance |
| Insurance before payment | HBC cover over $20,000, certificate before any deposit | Domestic building insurance over $16,000, certificate of currency before the deposit |

Queensland runs its own regime through QBCC. The clearest software trigger there is insurance: most residential building work valued at more than $3,300, including materials, labour and GST, must be covered by the Queensland Home Warranty Scheme. Confirm the current Queensland contract and payment rules with QBCC or your lawyer before configuring claims for Queensland jobs.

Victoria's Consumer Affairs guidance sets out the usual percentages:

| Contract to | Base | Frame | Lock up | Fixing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Build to lock up | 20% | 25% | Balance | Not applicable |
| Build to fixing | 12% | 18% | 40% | Balance |
| Build all stages | 10% | 15% | 35% | 25% |

The remaining percentage falls at completion. CAV also says owners must notify the Building and Plumbing Commission within 28 days if the revised final cost of work rises by $15,625 or more, for permits issued from 1 July 2019. NSW's guidance stresses that progress payments should match work actually done, not time on the job.

A claims module built on these rules should:

1. Store the contract's payment schedule and state, with Victoria defaulting to the statutory stages.
2. Refuse to issue a claim for a stage until its completion evidence is attached, such as photos and the building surveyor's frame approval.
3. Block any deposit request until the insurance certificate is recorded against the job.
4. Generate the claim as an invoice in Xero or MYOB, and track payment back to the job.

## Home warranty and insurance records

**Insurance comes first, and the evidence must be on file before the first payment.** NSW says it is illegal for a contractor to ask for a deposit or other payment when Home Building Compensation cover is required unless the cover has been taken out and a certificate given to the homeowner. The Home Building Compensation Fund protects homeowners as a last resort if the builder can't complete work or fix defects because of insolvency, death, disappearance or licence suspension for not complying with a compensation order. In Queensland, the licensed principal contractor collects the premium from the owner and pays it to QBCC.

Software should hold the certificate or policy number, cover amount and dates against each job, and stop the deposit claim if anything is missing.

## Variations and selections the homeowner can follow

**A variation is where trust in a build is won or lost, and NSW sets clear rules for the paperwork.** Variations must be in writing, attached to the contract and signed by both parties, and the document must explain the cost implications and show how the price change was calculated, not just a dollar amount. Before the work starts, the homeowner should receive a description, any plans, the extra cost and any extra time. If the reason for a variation is the builder's fault, the homeowner doesn't pay for the rectification.

A good variation flow on a phone looks like this: the supervisor raises it on site with photos, the office prices it with the calculation shown, the homeowner reviews the cost and time impact in the portal, signs, and the variation joins the contract and the next claim automatically. Selections work the same way, with deadlines tied to the programme so a late tile choice becomes a priced variation rather than a delay nobody explained.

## Client portals that cut "what's happening on site?" calls

**The portal's job is to answer the homeowner's questions before they ask.** The features that matter:

- Progress photos posted by the supervisor, grouped by week and stage.
- The next two or three milestones and what the homeowner needs to do for each.
- Selections due, variations awaiting approval and claims awaiting payment.
- Documents: contract, plans, insurance certificate, approvals and, at handover, warranties and manuals.
- A message thread per job, so conversations don't scatter across texts and email.

## Defects and maintenance after handover

**Handover starts a warranty period that can run for years, and the records need to outlast the job file.** A defects and warranty tracker logs each item the homeowner reports, with photos, the trade responsible, the response and the closure evidence, and keeps the original insurance and inspection records with the job. If the homeowner commissions an independent inspection, the findings can be imported against the same items; our [building inspection software](/solutions/building-inspection-software) page covers the inspector's side.

## Xero or MYOB job costing, and where it breaks

**Xero and MYOB are fine ledgers for builders. They strain when job costing needs stage, cost code and variation detail they weren't designed to hold.** The usual fix is to keep the detail in the job system and push summarised, correctly coded transactions to the ledger, with claims as invoices and supplier bills coded to job and stage. Our [Xero and MYOB integration](/services/xero-myob-integration) work does exactly this. The rule is one source of truth for money (the ledger) and one for progress (the job system).
