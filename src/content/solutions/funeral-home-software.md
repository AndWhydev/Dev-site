## Why do US funeral platforms fit Australian funeral homes badly?

**Because the hard parts of running an Australian funeral home are state rules, and North American software is not written around them.** Platforms such as Passare, Gather and FrontRunner (now part of Tribute Technology) are capable case management tools, with first call capture, case workflows, contracts, websites and livestreaming. What they cannot know out of the box is that NSW forbids writing "complimentary" in place of a price, that Victoria wants your complaint handling procedure stated on every quotation, or that your death registration goes through a state registry portal with its own fields and order.

So directors end up with the platform for case tracking, Word templates for quotes, a spreadsheet for prepaid contracts and the registry website open in another tab. That is where errors creep in, and where a well designed local system pays for itself. It need not replace everything. Often the best result is a smaller platform plus one custom piece that ties the catalogue, quote and paperwork together. Our [build vs buy guide](/guides/build-vs-buy-software) covers the general test.

## What does the price disclosure law actually require of your software?

**One source of truth for prices, published in every place you do business, and an itemised quote drawn from it.** The NSW Funeral Goods and Services Information Standard, in the Fair Trading Regulation 2019, applies to anyone who arranges the collection, transport, storage, preparation or embalming of bodies, including death doulas and funeral brokers. It was updated in 2020 to require prices to be displayed at your premises, on your website, on social media if you have no website, and when you visit a family at home.

| Rule | NSW (information standard) | Victoria (Funerals Act 2006) |
|---|---|---|
| Where prices must appear | Every place of business, website, social media if no website, home visits | Website and a prominent position at business premises |
| What the price list covers | Transport, storage, refrigeration plate, coffins or price range, care and preparation, viewing, service and venue, burial or cremation | Similar items, plus mortuary and crematorium location and owner |
| Packages | Show the least expensive burial or cremation package | State which package is least expensive |
| Disbursements | Show the cost of disbursements | Reasonable estimate of certificates, permits and third party fees |
| Before any agreement | Itemised quote with the total payable | Itemised statement of goods, service fees and disbursements, stating your complaint procedure |
| Banned shortcuts | No combined "total price" items; no "included", "complimentary" or "no charge" in place of a price | Prices must reflect the maximum you charge |

Software consequences follow directly. The catalogue needs every item as its own line with its own price, because NSW does not allow you to bundle two required items into one total. The website price page should be generated from the same catalogue as the quote, so they can never disagree. And the quote template needs state aware wording, since a home in Albury and one in Wodonga answer to different rules. Other states have their own requirements; check with the regulator in each state you operate in.

## From first call to final invoice: the arrangement workflow

**The arrangement meeting is where most of the information is captured, and most of it is needed again by someone else within hours.** A typical case moves through these steps, each with data the next one depends on:

1. **First call.** Name of the deceased, place of death, next of kin, whether the death has been referred to the coroner, and transfer timing.
2. **Transfer and mortuary.** Who collected, when, where the person is held, and identification checks.
3. **Arrangement meeting.** Burial or cremation, service type and venue, coffin, viewing, celebrant, music, notices and flowers, built as an itemised quote while the family is in the room.
4. **Family approvals.** The quote, the authority to proceed and any changes, signed electronically or on paper and stored against the case.
5. **Paperwork.** Medical certificate cause of death or coroner's disposal order, the death registration statement, and cremation or burial documents.
6. **Service day.** Run sheet, staff, cars, livestream and order of service.
7. **Invoice and payment.** Issued from the agreed quote, with disbursements reconciled, and posted to accounting.

A custom system earns its keep by entering each fact once. The arrangement screen builds the quote from the catalogue, the approved quote becomes the invoice, and the invoice syncs to accounting through [Xero or MYOB integration](/services/xero-myob-integration). Many funeral homes run this today on paper forms and spreadsheets; our guide to [replacing Excel or Access with a web app](/guides/replace-excel-or-access-with-a-web-app) describes that kind of move.

## Death registration and certificates

**Registration is a state process, and in NSW funeral directors do it through BDM's eRegistry portal.** The Registry says eRegistry lets directors attach scanned documents, register a death and order official death certificates at the same time. Interstate directors may get access if they register at least 10 deaths in NSW a month, and others email the death registration statement and either the medical certificate cause of death or the coroner's disposal order.

Two time limits matter for workflow design. A funeral director can order a death certificate on behalf of the family only within 30 days of registration, and can request corrections on the family's behalf within 30 days using the Registry's form. After that, only next of kin, the executor or their solicitor can apply for the certificate.

We have not found a public API for eRegistry, so a sensible build does not try to submit automatically. It holds the registration information in the order the portal asks for it, flags missing items before a staff member logs in, and tracks registration, certificate orders and the 30 day windows on a dashboard. Cremations add another layer of paperwork under state public health rules, which a checklist per state can prompt for.

## Prepaid funerals: a register, not a folder

**Prepaid funeral contracts are financial obligations that may sit open for decades, and the law expects a proper register.** Consumer Affairs Victoria says Victorian providers must disclose in writing where and how the money will be invested before the contract is signed, invest cash payments within three business days and other payments within seven, keep a register of every prepaid contract, and keep related records for three years after the funeral. NSW regulates prepaid funeral funds under the Funeral Funds Act 1979, with returns and auditor's reports lodged with the regulator.

A useful prepaid module records each contract, the person it covers, the payments and investment details, and the service promised. It raises a task when a payment arrives so the investment deadline is not missed, produces the register on demand, and, when the person dies, opens a case with the prepaid service already attached. Trust and investment rules vary by state and are a matter for your accountant and lawyer; the software makes the obligations visible.

## Family portals, tributes and webcasts

**Families increasingly expect to approve details, share a tribute and join a livestream from their phone.** Specialist tribute and streaming platforms do the public parts well. A custom family portal is worth building when you want arrangements, approvals, payments and the tribute page in one place under your brand, with each family seeing only their own case.

Privacy needs care. The portal holds information about the person who died and their family, and tribute pages are public by default on many platforms. Ask the family before anything is published, let them choose who can see it, and remove it when they ask.

## What does it cost?

**Indicative market ranges run from about $20,000 for a family portal to $100,000 for a full arrangement to invoice system (AUD, ex GST).** Conduct's cost guide puts mid sized Australian agencies at roughly $150 to $330 an hour, which is the basis for these planning figures.

| Build | Indicative range (AUD, ex GST) | What drives the cost |
|---|---|---|
| Family portal with tribute page and webcast links | $20k to $50k | Branding, approvals, payments, streaming provider |
| Prepaid contract register and reporting | $20k to $45k | Number of states, migration of existing contracts |
| Arrangement to invoice system with state price list, quotes and BDM paperwork prompts | $40k to $100k | Number of states and locations, catalogue size, accounting integration |
