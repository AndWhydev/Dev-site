## Where does a property manager's week actually go?

**Most of a property manager's week goes on messages, not management: maintenance requests, arrears reminders, inspection bookings, owner questions and applications, each touching two or three systems.** The trust ledger is rarely the problem. The work between the ledger and everyone else is.

A typical week on a 150 to 200 property portfolio looks something like this:

- **Maintenance:** a tenant reports a leaking tap by email with no photo, the manager asks for one, chases a quote, waits for owner approval, books the plumber, chases the invoice and closes the job. Six touches, four people.
- **Arrears:** daily checks of who is behind, reminder messages, notices issued on the right day for the state, and notes for the owner.
- **Routine inspections:** scheduling, entry notices, the visit, then turning photos into a report.
- **Applications:** collecting, comparing and shortlisting applicants, then explaining the decision to the owner.
- **Owners:** questions about statements, repairs and rent reviews, often by phone after hours.

Of these, maintenance coordination and owner communication are where custom work usually pays first, because the platforms handle them least completely.

## Which core platform, and how open is it?

**PropertyMe, Console Cloud and PropertyTree are the trust accounting platforms most Australian rent rolls run on, with Ailo and Kolmeo as newer alternatives. The question that decides what you can build is how open each one is.**

| What you want to connect | What to check first |
|---|---|
| Maintenance triage and dispatch | Can the API create and update jobs, and attach photos and invoices? |
| Owner reporting across the portfolio | Can you read ledger, arrears and maintenance data in bulk, or only by export? |
| Applications from Snug, 2Apply or Ignite | Does the platform already have a connector, and what does it pass across? |
| Inspections from Inspection Express or Inspect Real Estate | Are reports linked back to the property record automatically? |
| Acquired rent roll on a different platform | What migration tools and import formats does the target platform support? |

PropertyMe is the clearest example of why this matters. At the time of writing it says third party API access requires an application and agreement to its terms, a reasonable mutual client base and a significant benefit to its users, is granted solely at its discretion, and carries a minimum fee of $1,000 AUD plus GST a month. It also says it is not currently accepting applications for safety and compliance integrations. A custom build that assumes open API access can stall at that step, so we confirm access before scoping.

## What has to stay in the ledger?

**Trust money, bonds, rent receipts, disbursements and the audit trail belong in the core platform, and nothing custom should keep a second copy of the money.** Trust accounts are audited every year. Service NSW states that real estate trust account audit results must be lodged with NSW Fair Trading by a registered auditor through the Auditor's Report Online portal, and that it is the licensee's responsibility to make sure that happens by the due date. Queensland requires an annual audit by a qualified auditor and lodgement within 4 months of the end of the audit period.

So the design rule is simple. Custom tools read from the ledger for reporting, and write to it only through the platform's own supported functions, such as raising a bill against a property. They never calculate balances themselves.

## Bonds and state portals

**Each state holds rental bonds centrally, and the agent's systems need to track each lodgement and claim, even if the portal itself sits outside your platform.**

- **NSW:** bonds go to NSW Fair Trading. Agents must register with Rental Bonds Online and offer it as the first option, but can't require a tenant to use it, and can be fined $2,200 if they do. Where a claim is made without the tenant's consent, the supporting documents such as condition reports and quotes must reach the tenant within 7 days.
- **Victoria:** bonds go to the Residential Tenancies Bond Authority through RTBA Online. Consumer Affairs Victoria has fined agents for failing to lodge bonds.
- **Queensland:** bonds go to the Residential Tenancies Authority through its web services.

A small amount of software helps a lot here: a list of every tenancy where the bond is collected but not yet lodged, with days elapsed, and every open claim with the date documents were sent.

## Maintenance without the phone tag

**A maintenance layer that sits over the platform can turn six touches into two: the tenant's report arrives complete, and the job moves forward without the manager relaying messages.** This is the build we see the strongest case for in most rent rolls.

1. The tenant reports through a form, SMS or chat that insists on location, photos and a description.
2. The request is classified by urgency, with urgent repairs under the state's tenancy law flagged for immediate action.
3. If the likely cost is within the owner's approved limit, a work order goes to the preferred tradie; if not, the owner gets an approval request with the photos.
4. The tradie gets a job link to confirm, book access and upload the invoice, without needing a login to your platform.
5. Invoice and completion photos attach to the property record, and the bill is raised in the ledger for payment.

Tools such as Tapi already cover parts of this. Custom work makes sense when you want your own rules, your own tradie network, or a combined view across offices. It is a standard [workflow automation](/services/workflow-automation) job built on the platform's [API](/services/api-development).

## Rental reform changes your systems must reflect

**NSW and Victoria have both changed tenancy law in ways that reach into templates, notices and reporting.**

| Change | Where | What the software has to do |
|---|---|---|
| No grounds terminations ended, pet request rules eased (from 19 May 2025) | NSW | Update termination templates to require a ground; track pet requests and responses |
| A survey in Rental Bonds Online when a bond is claimed or released, including the reason if the landlord ended the tenancy, and re-letting restrictions after some terminations | NSW | Record who ended each tenancy and why, and block re-letting during any restriction period |
| Offence to rent out a property that doesn't meet minimum standards | Victoria | Store minimum standards evidence per property before it is advertised |
| Standard application form, ID only from the preferred applicant, altered image disclosure (from early 2027) | NSW | Change application intake, delete ID documents on schedule, label edited photos |

On images, the NSW Government says the new law requires disclosure when photos in a rental advert have been digitally altered or AI generated in a way that could mislead, with penalties of up to $22,000. Any AI photo editing or virtual staging in your listing workflow needs a disclosure step built in. Sales advertising is covered by the general misleading conduct rules on our [real estate agency software](/solutions/real-estate-agency-software) page.

## What AI should draft, and what it must never decide

**AI fits the communication load: drafting, summarising, classifying and chasing. Decisions about people and money stay with the property manager.**

Useful, with a person approving where it matters:

- Drafting arrears reminders and routine entry notices from the platform data.
- Summarising a routine inspection's photos and notes into a draft report.
- Answering owner questions about statements from the ledger data, with sources.
- Taking after hours calls through an [AI voice agent](/services/ai-voice-agents) that logs urgent repairs and escalates real emergencies.

Never automated: choosing between applicants, approving work over the owner's limit, issuing termination notices, and telling a tenant or owner what the law requires of them.

## Typical builds and indicative costs

**Most rent roll builds are focused layers over the platform, sized at the low to middle end of the mid complexity range for Australian custom software.** Expeed's 2026 cost survey puts mid complexity systems at $40,000 to $150,000 and simple internal tools at $15,000 to $40,000.

| Build | Indicative range (AUD, ex GST) | Main drivers |
|---|---|---|
| Maintenance triage and tradie dispatch over the platform | $25k to $60k | API access, approval rules, tradie app |
| Owner portal and portfolio reporting | $30k to $80k | Data sources, report types, owner logins |
| Rent roll migration and data clean up after an acquisition | $10k to $35k | Source system, data quality, volume |

Add any platform API fees, hosting and maintenance of roughly 15 to 20% of the build a year. For portal cost detail, see the [customer portal guide](/guides/customer-portal-development).
