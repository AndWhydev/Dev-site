## Why does a bookkeeping practice need different software to an accounting firm?

**An accounting firm's work is deep and periodic: a set of accounts, a tax return, advice. A bookkeeping practice's work is shallow and constant: the same twenty tasks in every client file, every week.** Software that helps one is not the same as software that helps the other.

An accountant opens a file in June and spends a day in it. A bookkeeper with 60 clients opens 60 files every week to reconcile, chase receipts, run payroll and get BAS ready. The cost is not any single task. It is the opening, checking and remembering across files, and the risk that one client slips through. That is why the most useful build for a practice is usually a view across every file, not another tool inside each one.

If your practice also does year end accounts and tax, our page on [AI for accounting firms](/solutions/ai-for-accounting-firms) covers that side.

## What does the usual stack do, and where are the seams?

**Each tool in a bookkeeper's stack is strong inside one client file; the seams are between files and between tools.**

| Tool | What it does well | Where it stops |
|---|---|---|
| Xero, MYOB, QuickBooks | Ledger, bank feeds, bank rules, payroll, BAS | Each file is separate; no practice wide exception list |
| Dext, Hubdoc | Reading receipts and bills, supplier defaults, publishing to the ledger | Shows what was captured, not what is missing |
| ApprovalMax, Lightyear | Approval workflows, purchase orders, bill matching | Approvals per organisation, not a practice dashboard |
| Karbon, Xero Practice Manager | Jobs, deadlines, client communication | Knows the job is due, not whether the file is ready |
| Employment Hero and ledger payroll | Pay runs and super | Little cross client view of super timing |

The common gap is a question no single tool answers: across every client, what is unreconciled, uncoded, unapproved or overdue right now?

## What does month end look like across 60 files?

**A month end checklist only helps if it is filled in from the file data, not ticked by memory.** Here is a checklist a cross client tool can run automatically each morning, file by file:

1. Bank accounts reconciled to the last business day, with the count and value of unreconciled lines.
2. Transactions sitting in suspense or uncategorised accounts.
3. Receipts and bills in Dext or Hubdoc not yet published.
4. Bills waiting for approval longer than your agreed limit.
5. Pay runs completed, and super paid against each one.
6. GST codes that differ from the supplier's usual code.
7. Accounts receivable over 60 days, for clients who pay you to chase.
8. Locked periods and lock dates set after BAS lodgement.

Each item becomes a column in a dashboard, each client a row, with the worst files at the top. Staff start the day on the red cells, not on opening files. The bookkeeper still does the work; the system tells them where.

## What did Payday Super change for payroll clients?

**From 1 July 2026, employers pay super guarantee for each payday rather than quarterly, and the contribution must reach the employee's fund within 7 business days after payday, with longer allowed in some cases such as new employees.** The ATO also confirms the Small Business Superannuation Clearing House has closed, so clients that used it had to move to another provider.

The ATO's own changeover example shows the new rhythm. An employer paying wages on Monday 6 July 2026 owed that super in the fund by Wednesday 15 July, while the final June quarter payment under the old system was due by 28 July. Missing a quarterly deadline meant lodging a super guarantee charge statement.

| Before 1 July 2026 | From 1 July 2026 |
|---|---|
| Super paid quarterly, by 28 days after quarter end | Super paid for each payday |
| Small Business Superannuation Clearing House available | SBSCH closed; alternative provider needed |
| One super deadline per client per quarter | One deadline per pay run, within 7 business days |

For a practice running weekly and fortnightly payroll for many clients, deadlines multiply. A check that reads each pay run, finds the super payment and confirms it cleared within the window is a small build with a clear purpose. It sits naturally on the [Xero and MYOB integration](/services/xero-myob-integration) layer.

## Which TPB Code duties touch your systems?

**The Code of Professional Conduct applies to every registered BAS agent, and the Tax Agent Services (Code of Professional Conduct) Determination 2024 added 8 obligations that reach into how practices keep records and handle data.** The Determination was registered on 2 July 2024. Its obligations applied from 1 July 2025 for practitioners with 100 or fewer employees at 31 July 2024, and from 1 January 2025 for larger ones.

The duties most relevant to software:

- **Client records.** Under section 30 of the Determination, records must correctly record the BAS services provided to each client, including former clients, be kept for at least 5 years, and show the nature, scope and outcome of the service and the information considered.
- **Confidentiality.** You must not disclose client information to a third party without permission or a legal duty. That governs which cloud and AI services can see client data.
- **Supervision and quality management.** Work done by staff, or produced by a tool, still needs proper supervision by the registered agent.
- **Reasonable care.** Automated suggestions need a human check before lodgement.

A separate change to note: from 1 July 2026, AUSTRAC regulates certain higher risk services provided by registered tax practitioners. The TPB says it applies to designated services, so check whether any of yours are in scope.

## Where does AI help, and where must a person sign off?

**AI works best as a second pair of eyes: suggesting codes from history, explaining why, and flagging transactions that break a client's pattern.** It is a poor substitute for the agent's judgement on GST treatment or a payroll classification.

Practical uses:

- Coding suggestions for unmatched bank lines, with the reason shown (same supplier coded to rent 14 times this year).
- Anomaly flags: a duplicate bill, an amount ten times the usual, GST on a supplier that never charges it.
- Draft client emails asking for missing receipts, listing the exact transactions.

Every suggestion is accepted or rejected by a person, and the decision is logged. That record supports the TPB's reasonable care and record keeping duties. ## What do these builds cost, including the API?

**Most practices need one tool across all their files, built on the ledger APIs, at the simple to lower mid complexity end of the market.** These are indicative AUD ranges, ex GST, not quotes. Expeed publishes $15,000 to $40,000 for simple internal tools and $40,000 to $150,000 for mid complexity systems.

| Build | Includes | Indicative range |
|---|---|---|
| Cross client exception dashboard | Xero or MYOB connections, nightly checks, ranked client list | $20,000 to $50,000 |
| Month end workflow and checklist tool | Checklist driven by file data, sign off per client, practice tool sync | $15,000 to $40,000 |
| AI coding review assistant | Suggestions with reasons, anomaly flags, accept and reject log | $20,000 to $50,000 |

Allow for API running costs. Xero prices its developer plans by connected organisations: Starter allows 5 at no charge, Core allows 50 for $35 AUD a month, and Plus allows 1,000 for $245 AUD a month, ex tax, at the time of writing, with higher tiers adding prerequisites such as app certification and security assessments. A 60 client practice sits on Plus. MYOB offers its Business API to registered developers. Our [workflow automation cost guide](/guides/workflow-automation-cost) explains the other drivers.
