## What do churches need that a generic CRM misses?

**A church database is a people system, a volunteer compliance system and a giving ledger at once, and generic CRMs only do the first well.** A sales CRM can store contacts and send email. It does not know that a youth leader's Working with Children Check expires in March, that one family gives to the building fund and another to general offerings with different tax treatment, or that the Sunday roster needs two cleared adults in every children's room.

Church management products exist for this reason. Elvanto (a Tithe.ly company), Planning Center, Tithe.ly's own tools and Pushpay all cover members, groups, rosters, check in and giving to varying degrees. For one congregation, one of them is almost always the right answer, and we would say so. Our [build vs buy guide](/guides/build-vs-buy-software) explains the test.

Where they fall short is above the congregation. A denomination, diocese or network of 40 parishes needs to know, centrally, whether every parish has verified every worker's check and completed its safe ministry training. Each parish may run a different product, or the same product in separate accounts. That central view is the realistic custom build, and it is the one this page focuses on.

## Volunteers, WWCC numbers and safe ministry training

**In NSW, an organisation that engages people in child related work must register with the Office of the Children's Guardian, verify each person's check online and keep a record of every verification.** The OCG says verification is a legal requirement with a fine for not doing it, that the check lasts 5 years, and that you verify again when a worker renews. Verifying also links the worker to your organisation in the OCG's continuous monitoring, so you are told if they are later barred.

Religious organisations carry extra duties. The OCG says religious bodies have been in the Reportable Conduct Scheme under the Children's Guardian Act 2019 since March 2020 and in the Child Safe Scheme since February 2022, and that since March 2020 people in religious ministry, or providing religion based activities to children, are mandatory reporters. Other states have their own check schemes and child safe standards, so a national denomination is tracking several regimes.

A safeguarding tracker for a denomination typically holds:

| Record | Why it matters | Typical source |
|---|---|---|
| Role and whether it is child related | Decides who needs a check | Parish ministry lists |
| Check number, state and expiry | Renewal reminders | Volunteer, then verified |
| Verification date and who verified | Legal record of verification in NSW | OCG employer portal |
| Safe ministry training and refresher dates | Denominational policy and child safe standards | Training provider or course records |
| Reportable conduct and incident records | Restricted access, strict timeframes | Professional standards office |

The design rules are about access. Parish administrators see their own people. The professional standards office sees incidents; ordinary administrators never do. Every view and change is logged.

## Giving: platforms, fees and when a receipt is tax deductible

**A gift is tax deductible only if it goes to an organisation or fund with deductible gift recipient (DGR) status and meets any gift conditions.** The ATO says you can check status on ABN Lookup, that not all charities are DGRs, and that from 1 July 2026 the $2 minimum for claiming a deduction on gifts to DGRs has been removed, backdated to gifts made from 1 July 2024.

For churches this creates a practical risk. Many congregations take general offerings, which are usually not deductible, and also run DGR endorsed funds, such as a school building fund or a welfare arm that is a public benevolent institution. If the giving platform puts both into one stream and issues one annual statement, a member may claim a deduction they are not entitled to.

A giving setup that handles this well:

1. Lists each fund separately, with its DGR status recorded.
2. Issues receipts only for deductible funds, worded to meet the ATO's requirements.
3. Gives annual statements that separate deductible from non deductible amounts.
4. Reconciles platform payouts and fees into the accounting system per fund.

Most church platforms can do much of this if configured carefully. Custom work tends to be the accounting side, such as syncing several parishes' giving into a central ledger.

## ACNC reporting and Basic Religious Charity status

**A Basic Religious Charity is exempt from some ACNC reporting, but the status depends on six criteria that can change year to year.** The ACNC says a Basic Religious Charity does not have to answer the financial questions in its Annual Information Statement, submit annual financial reports or comply with the Governance Standards. It must still lodge the Annual Information Statement each year, notify changes and meet the External Conduct Standards if it operates overseas.

The criteria include being registered only with the subtype of advancing religion, not being incorporated as a company or under a state associations act, not reporting as part of an approved group, and not being a DGR as a whole. Two of them are numbers a system can track:

- **Government grants.** A charity that receives more than $100,000 in government grants in the reporting period, or in either of the previous two, is not a Basic Religious Charity.
- **DGR funds.** A charity with DGR endorsement for some funds can stay a Basic Religious Charity only if total revenue from those funds is under $500,000 for the period.

A denomination's reporting tool can flag any parish approaching either threshold, well before the Annual Information Statement is due.

## Rosters, service planning and CCLI reporting

**Rosters and worship planning are where off the shelf products are strongest, so build here only for cross parish reporting.** Planning Center and Elvanto both handle service plans, song lists and volunteer rosters. CCLI's Church Copyright Licence covers projecting lyrics, printing songs and recording services, and CCLI now offers automatic reporting of songs used, so check your planning tool's integration before writing code.

A custom piece makes sense when the safeguarding tracker and the roster need to talk: for example, refusing to roster someone into a children's ministry slot if their check has not been verified. That rule is simple to state and valuable to enforce, and few products enforce it across separate parish accounts.

## Livestreams, apps and member privacy

**Religious belief is sensitive information under the Privacy Act, so a church member list is not an ordinary contact list.** The OAIC says the Act applies to a not for profit with turnover over $3 million and in some other cases, and that collecting sensitive information generally needs consent. A large diocese or denomination is likely to be covered.

Practical consequences for apps and livestreams:

- Collect only what you need, and explain why at sign up.
- Get consent before showing anyone's name or photo in a public directory or on a stream.
- Keep prayer requests and pastoral notes in a separate, restricted area.
- Host member data in Australian cloud regions where you can.

The privacy fundamentals for smaller organisations are covered in our [Privacy Act guide for small business software](/guides/privacy-act-small-business-software), and the [AI for not-for-profits](/solutions/ai-for-not-for-profits) page covers using AI tools with sensitive data.

## What do these builds cost?

**Indicative market ranges run from about $20,000 for a safeguarding tracker to $300,000 for a denomination wide member and giving platform (AUD, ex GST).** Conduct's guide puts mid sized Australian agencies at roughly $150 to $330 an hour, which underpins these planning figures.

| Build | Indicative range (AUD, ex GST) | What drives the cost |
|---|---|---|
| WWCC and safe ministry training tracker across parishes | $20k to $60k | Number of states, parishes and data sources |
| Central giving reconciliation into the denomination's accounts | $25k to $70k | Platforms used, number of funds and entities |
| Denomination wide member, safeguarding and giving platform | $100k to $300k | Migration from parish systems, roles, integrations |

These are ranges for planning, not quotes, and platform subscriptions and hosting sit on top.
