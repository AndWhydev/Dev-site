## What does gym software actually have to do?

**Sell memberships, collect payments reliably, open the door for paid up members and let them book classes, in roughly that order of importance.** Class timetables get the attention in demos, but a gym's revenue runs on recurring direct debits, and most member complaints are about billing and cancelling.

| Job | Typical Australian setup | What goes wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Membership and sign up | Clubware, Hapana, PerfectGym, ABC Glofox, Mindbody, Gymmaster | Terms, fees and cancellation rules aren't shown clearly at sign up |
| Recurring billing | Ezypay, Debitsuccess or the platform's own payments | Failed payments, retries and freezes handled inconsistently |
| Door and turnstile access | Gantner, Salto and other access systems | Access not cut or restored promptly when status changes |
| Classes and appointments | The platform's booking module and member app | Waitlists and no shows not linked to retention data |
| Reporting | Platform dashboards, spreadsheets | Franchise and multisite views need manual work |

The platforms are good. Where gyms struggle is in the joins between them and in the rules about how memberships start and end.

## What must your sign up flow show?

**Every fee, the minimum term, how renewal works and exactly how to cancel, before the member commits.** NSW Fair Trading's guidance for fitness businesses recommends clear, plain language contracts with no unfair terms, a separate sheet listing all fees, an explanation of how to cancel or suspend and what that costs, and no pressure to sign on the spot. It also suggests offering a cooling off period or trial.

The legal backstop is the unfair contract terms law. The ACCC says that since 9 November 2023, proposing, using or relying on unfair terms in standard form contracts is banned and penalties apply. NSW Fair Trading's consumer guidance lists gym terms that may be unfair, including terms that make it hard or costly to cancel, raise fees without letting members cancel, charge excessive late fees, prevent cancellation for serious illness or injury, or require an unreasonably long notice period.

A sign up flow built for this records which version of the terms each member accepted, shows fees in one place, captures any cooling off end date, and emails a copy straight away. Since 1 October 2026 card surcharges are also banned on eftpos, Mastercard and Visa, so card fees can't be added at checkout.

## How do the 2027 subscription rules change cancellations?

**From 1 July 2027, most gym memberships become subscription contracts, and gyms must disclose the terms upfront and provide an easy way out.** The Unfair Trading Practices Act 2026 inserts a new division into the Australian Consumer Law. A subscription contract includes an indefinite membership with automatic payments, a fixed term that rolls over unless stopped, a free trial that becomes paid, and an introductory discount that steps up to a higher rate. Each must give the member a right to end it.

What a gym has to do, mapped to the Act:

| Requirement | Section | What the software needs |
|---|---|---|
| State that the offer is a subscription contract and disclose charges, period, renewal, notice and how to end it | 48D | Disclosure block shown next to the join button, stored with the accepted version |
| Give prescribed information while the contract runs (details to be set by regulations) | 48E | Scheduled member notices, ready to configure once regulations are made |
| Provide an exit that is easy to find, straightforward and only needs reasonably necessary steps | 48F | A visible cancel option in the app and portal, no forced phone call or visit |
| Offer an online exit where members joined online | 48F | Online cancellation that works end to end, including notice periods |

Design the exit as carefully as the sign up: confirm the end date and any final payment on screen, send a written confirmation, stop the direct debit schedule automatically, and keep the record. Retention offers are fine, but they can't become an obstacle course.

## How should direct debit failures be handled?

**Retry predictably, tell the member quickly, and decide in advance what happens to access.** Payment providers such as Ezypay offer failed payment handling, and most platforms record dishonours. The gaps are policy and data. Decide how many retries, at what interval, what fee if any (and whether it would survive an unfair terms test), when door access is suspended, and how a member updates their card. Then report failed payment churn separately from voluntary cancellations, because they need different fixes.

A practical test before you change platforms: export the last 12 months of dishonours and count how many members recovered after a retry, how many updated their card after a reminder, and how many simply left. If most losses happen after the first failure, better messaging and an easy card update link will recover more revenue than any new software. If members recover but then cancel within weeks, the problem is the membership itself, not billing.

## What does 24/7 access control need from your software?

**Access must follow membership status within minutes, and unstaffed hours need logs, alerts and clear safety procedures.** When a member pays, freezes or cancels, the door system should reflect it straight away through the platform's integration with Gantner, Salto or your access vendor. For unstaffed hours, gyms typically want entry logs per member, tailgating or forced door alerts, duress or emergency buttons that notify someone, and CCTV linked to entry times. If you scan photo ID at sign up to stop pass sharing, the OAIC says to scan only when sighting the ID isn't enough and to explain it in your privacy policy.

## What can a waiver cover at a gym?

**Only liability for death and personal injury, and never for significant injury caused by reckless conduct.** Section 139A of the Competition and Consumer Act 2010 allows suppliers of recreational services, which include sporting and similar leisure activities, to exclude the consumer guarantees for death, physical or mental injury and related conditions. It doesn't let a gym exclude other guarantees. Store the signed waiver version against each member and have your lawyer write it.

## How do franchise networks report across clubs?

**With a reporting layer that pulls each club's data into consistent definitions of members, revenue, churn and visits.** Hapana, for example, promotes multisite and franchise features with sub accounts, and other platforms have head office views. Franchisors often still need their own layer: royalties calculated from billing data, league tables across clubs, cohort retention by join month, and alerts when a club's failed payment rate jumps. That is a contained [data analytics](/services/data-analytics) build on top of platform exports or APIs.

## Can AI help with churn and member messaging?

**Yes, for ranking risk and drafting messages, with staff deciding who to contact and what to offer.** A churn model uses visits, bookings, payment failures, tenure and membership type to score each member each week. The output is a call list, not an automated cancellation trap. AI can also draft personalised check in messages for staff to approve. Keep health information out of models unless members have agreed and you have a clear reason to use it.
