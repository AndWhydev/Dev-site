## Which cleaning business are you?

**Commercial contract cleaning and residential cleaning share a verb and almost nothing else in software terms.** A contract cleaner with 60 sites wins work through tenders, keeps it by proving quality to facility managers, and carries the risk of underpaying a large casual workforce. A residential cleaner wins work through Google and ads, keeps it through reviews, and lives or dies on how fast a homeowner can get a price and book.

| | Commercial contract cleaning | Residential and end of lease |
|---|---|---|
| Who pays | Facility managers, property owners, schools, offices | Homeowners, tenants, property managers |
| What wins work | Tender price, compliance evidence, references | Instant quote, reviews, availability |
| What keeps it | Audit scores and SLA reports | Repeat bookings and referrals |
| Biggest risk | Underpayment, sham contracting, subcontractor failure | Low conversion, no shows, disputes over quality |
| Typical platforms | Swept, Otuvy (formerly CleanTelligent) | Jobber, ServiceM8 |
| Typical custom build | Audit and proof of service app, compliance layer | Quote engine and booking flow |

Many businesses do both. If so, build two front ends on one set of staff, sites and pay data, rather than forcing homeowners through a commercial system or the other way round. The rest of this page takes each side in turn, commercial first.

## How should site rosters handle the Cleaning Award?

**Build the roster around the Cleaning Services Award and each site's contracted hours, so the pay outcome is known when the roster is published, not discovered at pay time.** The Fair Work Ombudsman says the award covers businesses in the contract cleaning services industry, meaning cleaning services provided under a contract, including event cleaning, trolley collection, hygiene and pollution control and minor property maintenance incidental to cleaning. Its rules cover minimum rates, employment type, penalty rates for weekends and after hours, allowances such as toilet cleaning and using your own vehicle, and breaks.

Commercial cleaning shifts are short, early or late, and often split across sites in one evening. That is where generic rostering goes wrong: the travel between two sites, a short engagement that triggers a minimum, or a weekend penalty applied to the wrong part of a shift. Put the award logic in one tested rules engine, connect it to a rostering tool such as Deputy or to your cleaning platform, and route any shift outside the normal pattern to a person before it is paid. Have your payroll adviser sign off the interpretation first.

## How do you prove the clean?

**With an audit trail the facility manager can see: who cleaned, when, what was checked, and photos where it matters.** Proof of service is what turns a cleaning contract from a cost line into something a client renews.

What a proof of service app records on each visit:

1. Clock on and off at the site, with the cleaner told when location is captured.
2. A task checklist for the site and area, ticked as work is done, not at the end.
3. Photos for agreed items, such as washrooms and kitchens, time stamped and attached to the visit.
4. Issues found, such as damage, low stock or a hazard, sent to the right person.
5. Periodic quality audits by a supervisor, scored against the site specification.
6. A client view showing visits, audit scores and open issues by site and month.

Swept lists time tracking with GPS check ins, scheduling, inspections and client messaging, and Otuvy lists inspections, work orders, QR codes and a client portal. The custom work is usually a client facing SLA report in the format your biggest clients want, or a link into a facility manager's own maintenance system. ## Where do subcontractors and sham contracting come in?

**Subcontracting is common in cleaning, and it is where the legal risk concentrates.** The Fair Work Ombudsman lists underpayments, sham contracting and supply chain issues as focus areas in the cleaning industry. It explains that workers may be employed by the contract holder, a subcontractor or a labour hire provider, and that telling an employee they are an independent contractor, when the business knew or should have known otherwise, may be sham contracting, which is illegal.

State labour hire laws reach into cleaning too. Victoria's Labour Hire Authority treats commercial cleaning as a high risk industry: its standard licence conditions there restrict cash payments and the use of independent contractors and subcontractors, and require reporting on subcontractor use. South Australia licensed cleaning labour hire before 2026 and has covered all industries since 29 January 2026. If a subcontractor supplies workers that you direct, check whether the arrangement is labour hire; our [labour hire software page](/solutions/labour-hire-software) covers what licensing involves.

A subcontractor register should hold, for each one: ABN, insurances and workers' compensation, labour hire licence where relevant, the sites they cover, the workers they place there, and evidence of how those workers are paid. When any item lapses, the affected sites should show it.

## What will large clients ask about modern slavery?

**Large clients now ask cleaning contractors detailed questions because the Modern Slavery Act 2018 requires reporting entities, broadly those with AU$100 million or more in revenue, to report on modern slavery risks in their operations and supply chains and the actions taken.** Cleaning is a sector these clients flag. Some use the Cleaning Accountability Framework, which runs building certification, contractor prequalification and portfolio ratings, in their procurement.

The questionnaire usually asks the same handful of things:

- How workers are recruited, and whether any fees are charged to them.
- Pay rates against the award, and how hours are recorded.
- Whether work is subcontracted, and how subcontractors are checked.
- How workers can raise grievances, in their language.
- Visa holders, and how work rights are checked.

A system that holds rosters, timesheets, pay, subcontractor records and grievance logs per site can answer these from data, with reports you export rather than write.

## How should residential quoting and booking work?

**A homeowner should get a firm price and a booked time in under two minutes, on a phone, without talking to anyone.** End of lease cleaning is the clearest case: price depends on bedrooms, bathrooms, condition, carpets, windows, ovens and extras, and the customer wants certainty because their bond depends on it.

The quote engine holds your pricing rules, not a flat rate. The booking step shows real availability from your team's calendar, takes a deposit or full payment, and sends confirmation and reminders. Re clean promises, if you offer them, need clear terms; the ACCC's guidance on unfair contract terms applies to standard form consumer contracts, and penalties have applied since 9 November 2023. ## What about consumables and stock per site?

**Consumables are a small cost per site and a large cost across a portfolio, and they are often the first thing a client complains about.** Track stock issued to each site against the contract, let cleaners report low stock from the same app they use for checklists, and include consumables in the per site margin report, so you know which contracts actually make money.
