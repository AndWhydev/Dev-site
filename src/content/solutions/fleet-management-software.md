## Telematics, fleet admin and maintenance: which job is actually broken?

**Most light vehicle fleets already have telematics; the gaps are in fleet administration and maintenance, which still run on spreadsheets.** It helps to separate the three jobs before buying or building anything.

| Job | What it covers | Typical tools | Common gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Telematics | Location, trips, speed, harsh events, engine data | Teletrac Navman, MTData, Geotab, EROAD, Webfleet, Linxio | Data stays in the vendor portal |
| Fleet administration | Vehicle register, allocation, FBT, tolls, fuel cards, fines, leases | Spreadsheets, Fleetio, finance system | Reconciliation and tax reporting |
| Maintenance | Services, tyres, registration, defects, inspections | Fleetio, dealer portals, telematics add ons | Joining odometer data to schedules and costs |

MTData, for example, markets Australian designed telematics and fleet software for Australian businesses, and the larger telematics vendors all offer trip logs and maintenance reminders. Where they stop is finance: who pays for what, which cost centre carries it, and what goes on the FBT return. That is where custom work tends to earn its place.

This page is about utes, vans and cars. If you run heavy vehicles, fatigue, mass and Chain of Responsibility rules apply, and our [logistics software page](/solutions/logistics-software-development) covers them.

## What does the ATO need from a digital logbook?

**A logbook for the operating cost method usually covers a continuous 12 week period that is representative of the car's business use, with specific details for every business journey.** The ATO's employer guide lists them: the dates the journey began and ended, odometer readings at the start and end, kilometres travelled, and the purpose. It is explicit that "business" or "miscellaneous business" is not enough as a purpose.

Odometer records of total distance are also needed, both for the 12 weeks and for the whole year. Without logbook and odometer records, the operating cost method is not available and the statutory formula method applies, which for most arrangements uses a 20% statutory percentage. Electronic records are treated the same as paper ones and must be kept for five years.

What this means for software:

- **Purpose capture.** A telematics trip log is not a logbook until each trip carries a real purpose. A driver app that prompts at trip end, with suggestions from the calendar or job system, fixes this.
- **Odometer, not just GPS.** Pull true odometer readings from the vehicle where the telematics unit supports it.
- **Representative period.** Let the fleet manager choose and lock the 12 weeks, and flag cars whose use has changed since.
- **Retention.** Store logbooks with the FBT records for the required period, outside the telematics vendor's rolling history.

## How do EVs change the paperwork?

**Electric cars can remove FBT on private use, but they add eligibility tests, reporting and charging calculations that someone has to keep straight.**

The ATO's conditions for the electric cars exemption are that the car is a zero or low emissions vehicle (battery electric or hydrogen fuel cell), first held and used on or after 1 July 2022, used by a current employee or their associates, and that luxury car tax has never been payable on it. Salary packaged cars are included. The benefit is still reportable, so values flow to employees' income statements.

Plug in hybrids stopped counting as zero or low emissions vehicles from 1 April 2025. The ATO lets the exemption continue for a PHEV only where the use was provided before that date under a financially binding commitment to keep providing it, and an optional extension does not count.

| Item a fleet system should store per EV | Why |
|---|---|
| Drivetrain type | Battery electric, fuel cell or plug in hybrid decides eligibility |
| Date first held and used | Must be on or after 1 July 2022 |
| LCT status at purchase or import | Any LCT payable removes eligibility |
| For PHEVs, the commitment date and terms | Grandfathering depends on a binding commitment before 1 April 2025 |
| Reportable value per FBT year | Feeds payroll reporting |
| Home charging method and percentage | Rate method or actual cost, chosen per vehicle per year |

Home charging is the fiddly part. For the FBT year starting 1 April 2026, the ATO's PCG 2024/2 sets an EV home charging rate of 5.47 cents per kilometre; earlier years used 4.20 cents. The ATO's own example applies a 75% home charging share to 10,000 kilometres, giving 7,500 kilometres at 5.47 cents, or $410.25. Software that holds odometer readings and public charging receipts can produce that number per car without a spreadsheet.

## How should tolls, fuel cards and fines be reconciled?

**Match every transaction to a vehicle, a driver and a moment in time, then decide who pays and how it is treated for tax.** Toll statements, fuel card files and infringement notices arrive in different formats on different cycles, and the reconciliation often falls to one person in finance.

Tolls need care for FBT. The ATO says that if FBT applies to an employee's private use of a car or eligible commercial vehicle and you pay their tolls, the tolls are generally subject to FBT as well. A minor benefits exemption can apply where the employee's total tolls for the FBT year are under $300 and treating them as a fringe benefit would be unreasonable, for example because they are infrequent and irregular.

A reconciliation tool typically:

1. Imports toll, fuel card and fine data on a schedule.
2. Matches each item to a vehicle by plate or card, then to the driver allocated at that time.
3. Uses telematics to test whether the trip was during work, and flags private use.
4. Routes fines for driver nomination within the issuing authority's deadline.
5. Posts costs to the right cost centre and summarises private tolls for the FBT return.

## Utes and vans: when is private use exempt?

**An eligible commercial vehicle can be exempt from FBT when private use is limited to home to work travel, incidental travel and other minor, infrequent and irregular use.** The ATO's eligible vehicle pages describe the vehicles (such as utes and vans designed to carry less than one tonne and not mainly for passengers) and point to PCG 2018/3 for its compliance approach. They suggest regular comparison of opening and closing odometer readings with expected home to work distance. That is an easy automated check: flag any vehicle whose monthly kilometres outside work hours exceed its expected commute.

## What should you know before tracking staff vehicles?

**Tracking is legitimate, but staff should know what is collected and when, and access to history should be narrow.** For employees, state workplace surveillance laws can require notice; NSW has a Workplace Surveillance Act 2005. Vehicles taken home raise the obvious question of after hours tracking. A privacy mode outside work hours that keeps only odometer totals usually satisfies both FBT needs and staff concerns. Get advice for each state you operate in.

## Is a work vehicle covered by WHS duties?

**Yes. A business's duty to ensure, so far as reasonably practicable, the health and safety of workers extends to the vehicles they drive for work.** Safe Work Australia's transport guidance frames this as the PCBU's duty and points to its model codes, including managing the risk of fatigue. For a light fleet, software usually supports pre start checks, defect reporting that takes a vehicle off the road until fixed, and incident records linked to the vehicle and driver.

## Where does AI help a fleet?

**Mostly in prediction and paperwork.** Maintenance prediction from engine fault codes and odometer trends, reading service invoices and fines into structured records, and plain English cost summaries for managers, such as which team's vans cost most per kilometre this quarter and why, are realistic. Using AI to judge whether a trip was private should stay a flag for a person to confirm.
