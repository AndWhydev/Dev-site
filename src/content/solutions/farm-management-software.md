## Which farm software covers what?

**No single Australian farm product runs a whole agribusiness: each of the main ones is strong in one enterprise and thin elsewhere.** That is fine. The job is to pick the right base products and fill the gaps deliberately.

| Product | Built for | Strong at | Usually leaves to you |
|---|---|---|---|
| AgriWebb | Livestock and grazing | Mob and individual animal records, mapping, grazing, tasks | Crew labour, processing or direct sales, cross property finance views |
| Agworld | Cropping and agronomy | Crop plans, agronomist collaboration, central records | Livestock, payroll, sensor data from your own hardware |
| Figured | Farm finance | Budgeting and forecasting on top of Xero or MYOB | Operational records |
| eNVD (Integrity Systems) | Livestock consignments | Electronic NVDs, NLIS transfers, offline QR sharing | Anything beyond the consignment |

A mixed operation with cattle, some cropping and a seasonal picking crew can easily end up with three subscriptions, a payroll system and a pile of spreadsheets. Custom software rarely replaces the subscriptions. It joins them, and it covers the records that none of them hold.

## What livestock records do you need to integrate?

**The Livestock Production Assurance NVD and the electronic eNVD are legal documents that travel with every animal and declare its food safety and treatment status.** Integrity Systems says producers must be accredited under the LPA program to use them, and that eNVDs are free and capture all livestock movement information digitally. The eNVD system also handles NLIS transfers.

Sheep and goat electronic identification is the big change. Integrity Systems says mandatory eID for sheep and goats comes into full effect nationally on 1 January 2027. From then, every movement of a tagged animal has a digital record, and so does every mistake.

Where custom software helps:

- **Single source of truth.** Pull animal, mob and treatment records from your livestock system so a consignment is never declared from memory.
- **Withholding period checks.** Block or warn on a sale when a treatment's withholding period hasn't expired.
- **Scanner integration.** Read eID tags from panel readers and wands into your own records, then reconcile against NLIS transfers.
- **Exceptions dashboard.** Show animals with missing tags, unreconciled movements or overdue treatments.

## How do you keep water licence and metering records?

**In NSW, large water users must install a pattern approved meter and a Local Intelligence Device that sends hourly water use data to WaterNSW daily.** The NSW water department groups users by pump size and entitlement:

| Group | Test | What applies |
|---|---|---|
| Large by pump size | Surface water pumps 500 mm or larger | Must be compliant now |
| Large by entitlement, inland | Total entitlement 100 ML or more | Must be compliant now |
| Large by entitlement, coastal | Total entitlement 100 ML or more | Must be compliant by 1 December 2026 |
| Smaller | More than 15 ML and less than 100 ML | Separate rules (check the department's metering guidance tool) |
| Exempt | 15 ML or less | Exempt from the metering rules |

Your telemetry data already goes to WaterNSW's Data Acquisition System. A custom dashboard adds what that doesn't: usage against your allocation per licence, alerts before you run short, a record of every temporary trade, and a view that combines meter data with soil moisture and weather so you decide when to irrigate. Other states have their own regimes, so we confirm the rules for each property's state during discovery.

## What chemical and spray records does the law require?

**In NSW, everyone who uses pesticides for commercial or occupational purposes, including farmers and market gardeners, must make a record within 48 hours of each use and keep it for 3 years.** The NSW EPA notes that licensed pesticide users must record extra information under the Pesticides Regulation 2017, and that if a label or APVMA permit requires a record, you must make it.

A paper spray diary fails in three ways: it is filled in late, it gets lost, and nobody can search it when the buyer's auditor or the EPA asks. A field app fixes all three if it:

1. Starts the record when the operator selects the product, with label rate and withholding period loaded.
2. Captures paddock or block from GPS and time from the device.
3. Records weather conditions at the time of spraying, either entered or pulled from a nearby station.
4. Locks the record after submission, with any later correction logged as an amendment.
5. Exports the records your buyers and quality assurance schemes ask for.

## How should software handle seasonal workers and piece rates?

**Under the Horticulture Award 2020, a pieceworker must be paid at least their hourly award rate, including the 25% casual loading for casuals, multiplied by the hours they worked that day.** The Fair Work Ombudsman says that since 28 April 2022 employers must give each pieceworker a signed piecework record before the work starts, listing the start date and time, the task, the piece rate, the hourly rate for their classification and a set statement about the minimum. A new record is needed before any piece rate change.

That makes hours as important as bins. Picking apps that count only units fail the floor test. A sound labour module:

- records clock on and clock off per worker per day, plus units picked
- calculates the hourly floor for each worker each day and tops up pay where piece earnings fall short
- issues and stores piecework records, including any revisions
- gives a worker a copy of their hours and piece rates on request

For workers under the Pacific Australia Labour Mobility (PALM) scheme, approved employers sign an Approved Employer Deed and follow its Guidelines, with monitoring that includes regular reporting and analysis of pay data. The PALM FAQ also says employers must appoint a welfare and wellbeing support person who meets all workers in person at least fortnightly. Software can hold those meeting records, accommodation details and approved wage deductions in one place. ## How do you build apps that work with no signal?

**Design the app to work fully offline and treat sync as a background task, not a feature.** On many properties the paddock has no coverage at all.

What that means technically:

- records stored locally on the phone or tablet, encrypted
- every record stamped with device time, GPS and user
- a sync queue that retries until the record reaches the server
- clear rules for conflicts, such as two people updating the same mob count
- sensors on LoRaWAN or similar low power networks reporting to a gateway at the shed, rather than relying on mobile data

## Where does AI add value with satellite, drone and sensor data?

**AI is useful for spotting where to look, not for deciding what to do.** Reasonable uses:

- Vegetation indices from satellite imagery to flag paddocks with falling feed or crop stress.
- Drone imagery to count stock, trees or plants.
- Forecasts of water use or yield from historical sensor and weather data.
- A plain language assistant that answers questions over your own records, such as "which mobs were drenched last quarter?"

Each output should lead to a person checking the paddock or the record. The data engineering underneath, joining imagery, sensors and farm records into one store, is usually the larger job. Our [data engineering service](/services/data-engineering) explains how we approach it.

## What do agtech builds cost?

**These are indicative AUD planning ranges, ex GST, not quotes.** Expeed publishes $40,000 to $150,000 as a typical range for mid complexity custom software in Australia.

| Build | Includes | Indicative range |
|---|---|---|
| Offline field app for spray and stock records | Offline capture, GPS, product library, withholding checks, sync, exports | $40,000 to $110,000 |
| Sensor and water dashboard | Meter and sensor ingestion, allocation tracking, alerts, weather join | $30,000 to $90,000 |
| Crew labour and piece rate module | Clock on, units, hourly floor top up, piecework records, payroll export | $30,000 to $80,000 |

Cost rises with the number of hardware types, the number of systems you integrate with and how many properties and enterprises the data model has to cover.
