## How do the PMS, channel manager and booking engine fit together?

**The PMS is the hotel's record of rooms, rates, reservations and folios; the channel manager pushes availability and rates out to OTAs; the booking engine takes direct reservations on your website.** In smaller properties these often come as one subscription from an all in one product, while groups tend to pair a PMS such as RMS Cloud, Mews, Cloudbeds or Oracle Opera Cloud with a dedicated channel manager like SiteMinder.

Around that core sit payments, door locks, housekeeping, revenue management, the restaurant POS and accounting. Each has its own integration, and that's where problems appear: a minibar charge that never reaches the folio, a function invoice raised outside the PMS, or a group whose three properties report on three different definitions of occupancy.

The rule we'd apply is simple: keep the PMS as the system of record, and build only at the edges. RMS describes an open API ecosystem as standard, Mews publishes a Connector API for partners, and Cloudbeds runs an API developer hub. Those interfaces are what make custom work possible without a risky PMS migration.

## Direct booking or OTA: what can a custom flow actually win back?

**A better direct booking flow wins back guests who already know you, not guests the OTA found for you.** Be honest about that split before you budget for it.

The commercial room to compete exists. After an ACCC investigation, Expedia (including Wotif) and Booking.com removed requirements, from 1 September 2016, for Australian accommodation providers to offer rates equal to or lower than other OTAs or their own offline channels, and to make all remaining inventory available. The ACCC said hotels could then offer lower rates by phone and walk in, and special deals to loyalty groups. Check your current contracts for what they say about your own website.

Where a custom direct booking front end helps:

- **Speed and clarity on mobile.** Fewer steps from date search to payment, with the total price visible throughout.
- **Member and repeat guest rates** tied to a login or a verified email, which OTAs can't see.
- **Packages** that combine a room with dinner, parking or a late checkout, priced from the PMS and the POS.
- **Corporate accounts** with negotiated rates and invoicing rules, booked without a phone call.

A useful worked test: if your average direct booking saves $40 in commission and a new front end costs $60,000 to build plus $8,000 a year to run, you need around 1,700 extra direct bookings in the first year just to break even on build and running costs. Some hotels clear that easily; many don't.

## What do Australian price display rules require?

**Advertise one total price that includes every unavoidable fee, and from 1 July 2027 show per transaction charges next to the base price.** The ACCC says the total must include taxes, duties and all unavoidable or preselected fees, shown as a single figure. Its own accommodation example is a serviced apartment charging a $7 per person cleaning fee: because at least one person stays on any booking, one cleaning fee should be in the advertised nightly price, with the full total shown as soon as the guest count is known.

Two further changes affect hotel checkouts:

1. **Card surcharges.** Since 1 October 2026, eftpos, Mastercard and Visa ban surcharges for paying by card, and the RBA says American Express, UnionPay and PayPal have also removed them. Booking engines that added a card fee at the last step need to absorb it into rates.
2. **Transaction based charges.** The Unfair Trading Practices Act 2026 commences on 1 July 2027. Any mandatory per transaction charge, such as a booking fee, must be displayed with the base price, including whether the base price already includes it.

A booking engine should therefore calculate totals from the PMS's fee rules at every step, not append fees at checkout. Victoria's short stay levy is a different matter: the State Revenue Office excludes commercial residential accommodation such as hotels, motels and hostels, so a hotel system doesn't need it, although a group that also lets apartments might. ## What should you stop storing about guests?

**Most hotels hold more identity data than they need, for longer than they need it, and the OAIC has said so plainly.** Its guidance on ID scanners says you can only scan a customer's ID if it is reasonably necessary for your functions, that you should not scan or copy an ID if sighting it would do, and that licences can carry sensitive information, which needs consent. Your privacy policy must explain any scanning, how the data is held and how long it is kept.

APP 11 then requires reasonable steps to protect the data, and to destroy it or remove identifying details once it is no longer needed. Practical rules for a hotel system:

- Record that ID was sighted (by whom, when, document type) rather than storing the image.
- If a scan is genuinely needed, keep it encrypted, outside the general reservation record, with automatic deletion.
- Use the payment provider's tokens instead of storing card numbers anywhere in your own systems.
- Restrict PMS exports. Many hotel data leaks start with a spreadsheet emailed to a third party.

If something does go wrong, the notification steps are covered in our guide to [the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme](/guides/notifiable-data-breaches-software).

## Where do pre arrival, upsells and digital check in fit?

**They sit between the booking and the front desk, reading and writing PMS data through its API.** A typical pre arrival flow emails the guest a link, confirms arrival time, offers upgrades or add ons priced from the PMS, collects any registration details the property needs, and issues a mobile key where the lock system supports it.

The traps are integration ones. Upsell revenue has to post to the right folio, not a separate payment page; room upgrades must respect inventory in real time; and if the PMS or lock vendor doesn't support a step, the guest app must fail back to the front desk gracefully. Ask your PMS vendor which endpoints are certified for partner use before designing anything.

## What can AI add to revenue management and guest service?

**AI helps most where a human currently answers the same question or reviews the same data every day.** Good starting points:

- Answering pre arrival questions (parking, check in times, dietary requests) from the hotel's own information, with handover to staff for anything unusual. Our [AI chatbot service](/services/ai-chatbot) covers how these are built and tested.
- Summarising guest reviews by theme across properties, so managers see recurring issues, not just star ratings.
- Forecasting F&B covers and housekeeping hours from bookings, events and history.
- Supplementing a revenue management system with local signals it doesn't see, with a person approving rate changes.

## How do multi property groups handle housekeeping and reporting?

**Groups usually need one reporting layer over several PMS instances, and a mobile housekeeping and maintenance app that every property uses the same way.** Room status, defects and lost property are often tracked on paper or in a chat group. A shared app that writes room status back to each PMS, with photos and job histories, gives the group consistent data. A reporting layer then standardises occupancy, ADR, RevPAR and non room revenue across properties, including F&B and events that sit outside the PMS.

## What do hotel software builds cost?

**Reporting and booking front ends sit in the mid range; guest apps with check in and mobile keys cost the most.** These are indicative AUD market ranges, ex GST, not quotes, consistent with the bands Expeed publishes for Australian custom software.

| Build | Indicative range |
|---|---|
| Housekeeping and maintenance app writing back to the PMS | $25,000 to $60,000 |
| Multi property reporting and revenue dashboard | $25,000 to $70,000 |
| Custom direct booking front end on a PMS API | $30,000 to $90,000 |
| Guest app with pre arrival, upsells and digital check in | $70,000 to $200,000 |

The biggest cost drivers are the PMS API's coverage, the number of properties and systems, and whether door lock and payment vendors support the flow you want.
