## Why do architects outgrow generic project tools?

**An architecture practice earns its fee in stages, so the question that matters every week is how much of each stage's fee has been used against how much of the work is done.** Generic task tools track to do lists. Timesheet apps track hours. Neither tells a principal that, for example, design development on a mid rise job has burnt 80% of its fee at 50% complete, or that a client variation hasn't been priced yet.

Three things make architecture different from other consulting work:

- **Fees are fixed by stage, work is billed by hours.** Concept, schematic design, design development, documentation, tender and contract administration each carry a budget, and the overrun usually appears in documentation.
- **Drawings are the deliverable and the evidence.** Revisions, issue dates and who checked each sheet matter for fees, for disputes and, in NSW, for regulated design declarations.
- **The architect carries registration duties.** Registration numbers on documents, insurance, continuing professional development and, on some buildings, statutory declarations all need records that sit outside the drawing tool.

## How should fee proposals turn into stage invoices?

**A fee proposal should become the project budget automatically, stage by stage, so that invoices, variations and forecasts all come from one number.** In most practices the proposal lives in a Word template, the budget is retyped into the practice tool and variations live in emails.

A sound flow looks like this:

1. The fee proposal is built from stage templates with hours by role and a fee per stage.
2. Acceptance turns the proposal into stage budgets in the practice tool.
3. Timesheets are coded to stage, and the project architect records percentage complete each fortnight.
4. Variations are priced, approved by the client and added as their own budget lines before work starts on them.
5. Invoices are raised per stage or per claim, pushed to Xero and reconciled back.

Where off the shelf tools fall short is usually step 3: a forecast that compares fee used with work complete across every live project, so a principal can see which jobs are drifting. That is a modest build over existing data.

## What does the NSW design practitioner scheme ask of your records?

**In NSW, regulated designs for class 2 buildings, and for new class 3 and 9c buildings since 3 July 2023, must be prepared and declared by a registered design practitioner and lodged on the NSW Planning Portal before building work starts.** Building Commission NSW says alteration and renovation work on existing class 3 and 9c buildings comes into the scheme on 1 July 2028. Mixed use buildings with a regulated part are caught too.

The details are what turn this into a records problem:

- A separate design compliance declaration is needed for each regulated design, even if you are registered in several classes. The multiple design form stopped being accepted after 5 March 2023.
- Declared designs must be construction issued, not shop drawings or drafts, and must carry the standard title block, which the Planning Portal reads to extract design data.
- If a variation touches a building element or performance solution after work starts, new regulated designs must be prepared before the varied work continues.
- Only the supervising designer who signs the declaration needs to be registered, so you need to know who supervised what.

A practice working on apartments can end up with dozens of regulated designs per project, each with versions, declarants and lodgement status. A register that links each design to its drawing revision, its declarant, the title block check and its Portal status is a small, valuable tool. It records the process; the declaration remains the practitioner's professional act.

## Which registration duties touch your documents and data?

**Architect registration rules create small, repetitive record keeping tasks that software can carry.** In NSW, clause 17(1) of the Architects Code of Professional Conduct requires the architect's registration number on stationery, presentations and construction documents, including drawings, specifications and schedules. Clause 15 requires professional indemnity insurance appropriate to the services provided. The NSW Architects Registration Board says architects should complete at least 20 hours of CPD each year, at least 10 of them formal, reported at renewal. Firms providing architectural services must nominate a responsible architect.

Registration itself rests on the AACA's National Standard of Competency for Architects, the 2021 version of which underpins the registration boards in every state and territory. Practical software tasks that follow:

- Title block templates that pull the right registration number and fail a check if it's missing.
- A CPD log per architect, with formal and informal hours, ready for renewal.
- An insurance register with renewal reminders and limits by project.
- A nominated architect record per entity if you run more than one.

## Which NCC edition applies to this project?

**It depends on the state and the date, and the two editions currently overlap.** The Australian Building Codes Board's adoption table shows NCC 2025 adopted on 1 May 2026 in Victoria, the ACT and Western Australia (the ACT and WA with 12 month transitions), and on 1 May 2027 in NSW and Queensland. The Northern Territory has not adopted it at the time of writing. A practice working across borders should record the applicable edition on each project at the point it is locked in, so specifications, details and energy calculations are checked against the right one.

## How can Revit and Archicad data reach your practice systems?

**Leave the model where it is and pull out the data you need: sheet lists, revisions, schedules and areas.** Autodesk's Design Automation API runs Revit and other Autodesk engines in the cloud, which lets a scheduled job open a model and extract sheet and schedule data without anyone exporting by hand. Archicad works with openBIM standards such as IFC, which gives a route to the same kind of data.

Useful connections include a drawing register that updates itself from the model, area schedules feeding fee checks on area based fees, and revision data feeding the regulated design register. ## Can AI find details and specifications from past projects?

**Yes, and it is one of the better uses of AI in a practice, as long as every answer shows its source and nobody treats it as a compliance check.** A practice with 15 years of projects has thousands of details, specification clauses and consultant reports. Search across them with retrieval, restricted by project and client where confidentiality applies, saves real documentation time.
