## Why speed and structure decide the software

**A pre purchase inspector sells one thing: a clear, defensible report delivered before the buyer has to commit.** In NSW, the cooling off period on a private treaty purchase starts at exchange and ends at 5pm on the fifth business day, and NSW Fair Trading points out that most consultants need 2 to 3 days' notice. Many inspections happen before exchange, but either way the window is short. The inspection, the write up, the buyer's reading and their decision all have to fit inside it.

So the software has two jobs. It has to make the report fast to produce, which means capturing everything once on site. And it has to make the report hold up, which means structure, evidence and wording that follow the standard and your professional indemnity insurer's expectations.

## What has to be in the report?

**Pre purchase building inspections in Australia are written to AS 4349.1, and timber pest inspections to AS 4349.3.** NSW Fair Trading's buyer guidance says there is an Australian Standard that pre purchase building reports must comply with, but it doesn't dictate style or content beyond that, and its agent guidance refers to building inspections as "usually AS 4349". The detail of each section comes from the standard itself, so a licensed copy is needed to configure any template properly.

NSW's buyer guidance describes what the inspector should check and what a report typically includes:

- All accessible parts: interior, exterior, roof space, under floor, roof exterior and the site, including garages, carports, sheds, separate laundries or toilets and small retaining walls.
- A summary of the building's condition relative to its age and type, and any major faults.
- Significant problems that need fixing.
- Recommendations for specialist assessment where needed: pest inspector, electrical or water authority, structural or geotechnical engineer, surveyor or solicitor.
- Limitations: areas not accessible, and the fact that the report isn't a certificate of compliance or a cost estimate.

A good template makes every one of those a section that cannot be left blank without a reason.

## Off the shelf apps, and why Word templates persist

**Many Australian inspectors still write in Word or Pages because their wording is their reputation, and generic apps don't let them keep it.** Spectora and HomeGauge are mature products, but they are structured around US inspection practice. Form builders such as Formitize are flexible but leave you to build the logic. Word templates let you keep your clause library and layout, at the cost of retyping site notes every night.

| Approach | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|---|
| Word or Pages template | Your wording, your layout, no subscription | Retyping, photo handling by hand, inconsistent across inspectors |
| US built inspection app | Mobile capture, delivery, scheduling | US structure and terms, limited AS 4349 alignment |
| Generic form builder | Flexible, offline forms | You build and maintain all the report logic |
| Custom app over your clause library | AS 4349 structure, your wording, offline, fast output | Upfront build cost and ongoing maintenance |

## On site capture that works without signal

**Roof spaces, subfloors and rural blocks often have no coverage, so capture must work offline and sync later.** A capture app built for inspectors usually needs:

1. The report structure loaded on the device before the inspection, by area and element.
2. Photo capture straight into the defect, with automatic numbering, location and timestamp.
3. Moisture and thermal readings attached to the defect they support, with the instrument recorded.
4. Your clause library searchable by element, so a defect is recorded as clause plus note, not free text.
5. Voice notes transcribed into the defect for later tidying.
6. Limitations recorded as you go, with photos of the obstruction.

## From site notes to a finished report in under an hour

**When capture follows the report structure, the write up is review, not retyping.** The inspector opens the draft, reads it top to bottom, adjusts severity and wording, checks the summary, and signs. The system generates the PDF with photos placed under each defect, the limitations section, and the standard terms, and sends it to the client with a link they can share with their conveyancer.

Version control matters. If a report is reissued, the original stays on file with a reason for the change. That record is what you will want if a finding is ever disputed.

## Licensing, insurance and complaints by state

**Who may inspect, and who handles complaints, depends on the state.** Two examples from official guidance at the time of writing:

| State | What the regulator says |
|---|---|
| NSW | Use a suitably qualified person such as a licensed builder, surveyor or architect, and make sure they have adequate insurance, particularly professional indemnity. |
| Queensland | QBCC accepts complaints about pre purchase building and termite inspections; the client must first raise concerns with the inspector in writing, allowing about 14 days, and QBCC may then inspect and report. |

If you work across states, check each regulator's current requirements before configuring licence fields and report terms. Software should store each inspector's licence numbers, insurance policy and expiry, and block report issue if either has lapsed.

## Bookings, agent referrals and NSW disclosure records

**Many jobs arrive through agents, and NSW rules make the paper trail matter on both sides.** NSW Fair Trading requires selling agents to make written records of any pre purchase reports they know of, including the inspection date, who requested the report, who prepared it with business contact details, whether the author holds professional indemnity insurance, and whether the report is available for purchase. Agents must disclose those records when a buyer asks for the contract, with penalties of up to $4,400 for a corporation.

That makes a clean booking system useful for inspectors too:

- Record who requested each report and who the client is.
- Record any referral source and its terms.
- Provide agents with the details they must record, in one standard message.
- Offer report resale to later buyers where your terms allow it, with the original client's position respected.

## Where AI helps, with the inspector's sign off

**AI is good at language and sorting, and that is exactly where inspectors lose evening hours.** Safe uses:

- Turning terse notes ("crack, NW bed, 3mm, stepped") into your standard defect wording from the clause library.
- Suggesting photo captions and grouping photos by area.
- Drafting the summary from the recorded defects.
- Checking the draft for empty sections, missing limitations or defects without photos.

Rules that should be built in: AI can't add a defect, delete a defect or change a severity rating. The inspector's original notes stay attached to every defect, and the final report records who approved it. This is a constrained form of [AI document processing](/services/ai-document-processing) where the source of truth is the inspector, not the model.
