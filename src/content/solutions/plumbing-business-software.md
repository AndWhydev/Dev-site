## Why is backflow testing a product of its own?

**Backflow testing is recurring, asset based work with a regulator at the end of it, which is a different shape from a plumbing job.** A blocked drain is a job: someone calls, you attend, you invoice. A testable backflow device is an asset that must be tested every year for as long as it exists, at a site you may not otherwise visit, with a report that goes to the water authority rather than only to the customer.

That difference matters once you hold a few hundred devices. Each one needs a due date, a reminder to the owner or property manager, a booking, a test by an accredited plumber, a report in the authority's format, and a record kept for audit. Miss the cycle and the customer gets a letter from their water authority, or worse.

Most plumbing businesses run this from a mix of recurring jobs, spreadsheets and calendar reminders. It works until the register grows past what one person can hold in their head.

## What does each water authority want back?

**Every water authority runs its own lodgement channel, so a business testing across service areas needs more than one output.** Two examples show how different they are:

| Authority | Registration | Testing | How results are lodged |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sydney Water | Devices registered on the Backflow Prevention portal immediately after installation | Annual, by a backflow accredited licensed plumber | Test reports submitted through the Backflow Prevention portal or Sydney Water Tap in, using the plumber's personal email and licence |
| Yarra Valley Water | Testable devices at the water meter | Every 12 months, by an accredited backflow plumber | Backflow Test Report Form, sent by email |

Sydney Water adds detail a register should capture: only accredited plumbers may commission, test and register containment devices; work must follow AS/NZS 3500.1, AS/NZS 2845 and the Plumbing Code of Australia; and plumbers should keep photos of serial numbers and test kits in case of audit. Zone and individual device reports don't go on Sydney Water's portal; Sydney Water directs those to Building Commission NSW.

For software, that means the device record should hold the authority, the hazard rating, the serial number photo, the test kit used and its calibration, and the submission status per authority. We didn't find a public API for either authority, so plan for software that prepares a complete report and tracks lodgement, with the accredited plumber submitting in the portal or by email.

## What paperwork does each state require for plumbing work?

**Each state has its own certificate or notice, deadline and channel, summarised here from regulator pages at the time of writing (November 2026).**

| State | What you lodge | When | Channel |
|---|---|---|---|
| NSW (Sydney, Illawarra, Blue Mountains, Newcastle, Hunter) | Notice of Work, Certificate of Compliance and Sewer Service Diagram | Notice before work starts; certificate at completion or before booking the final inspection; diagram at completion of drainage work | MyInspections Gateway, Building Commission NSW |
| NSW (elsewhere) | Combined Notice of Work and Certificate of Compliance | As required by the local plumbing regulator | Local council or other plumbing regulator |
| Victoria | Compliance certificate, for work over $750 including materials, labour and GST, and for listed work of any value such as below ground sanitary drains and Type A gas appliances | To the customer and lodged within five days of completion | VBA360, Building and Plumbing Commission |
| Queensland | Form 4 for notifiable work | Within 10 business days of invoicing; copy to the occupier within 20 business days of finishing | QBCC Portal ($36.88 online, $49.80 manual at the time of writing) |

Two quirks trip up generic software. Victoria's $750 threshold includes materials supplied by someone else, so the system has to know the full job value, not just your invoice. NSW sewer service diagrams have strict format rules, including official templates and scans rather than photos, so a phone snap attached to the job won't do.

## Where do job platforms lose the thread?

**Job platforms are built around jobs and invoices, and compliance falls through where the record needs to outlive the job.** simPRO, AroFlo, Fergus, ServiceM8 and Tradify all schedule, quote, invoice and capture forms and photos. Several support recurring or maintenance jobs, and AroFlo publishes help material on backflow prevention maintenance. Check how your platform handles these before you build anything:

1. A device register across many client sites, with hazard rating, serial number and authority per device.
2. Due dates that roll forward automatically after each passed test, and stay put after a failed one.
3. One report format per water authority, not one generic form.
4. A view for property managers or strata showing every device they own and its status.
5. Certificates drafted from job data in each state's format.

If your platform does three of these, configure it and stop. If it does none, a small register app reading from and writing back to the platform's API is usually cheaper than moving platforms. Our [customer portal guide](/guides/customer-portal-development) covers the property manager view.

## How should TMVs and other recurring services be handled?

**Thermostatic mixing valves and similar devices follow the same pattern as backflow: an asset, a test interval, a report and a record.** AS 4032.3:2022 sets requirements for field testing, maintenance and replacement of thermostatic mixing valves, tempering valves and related devices. Hospitals, aged care homes and childcare centres usually run TMV programs through their facilities team, and they expect contractor reports in their format.

The same register can hold backflow devices, TMVs, hot water units under maintenance and grease traps, each with its own interval and report template. Building one register for all recurring compliance work, rather than one tool per device type, is the main design decision. ## How do photos and drain camera footage become evidence?

**Evidence only protects you if it is attached to the right job and asset, timestamped, and still retrievable years later.** Before and after photos, pressure test results, drain camera footage and signed forms are your answer when a customer, builder or regulator disputes the work.

A checklist for evidence handling in any system:

- Photos and video saved against the job and, for devices, against the asset
- Capture time and location kept, not stripped on upload
- Large video stored outside the job platform if it charges for storage, with a link on the job
- Required photos enforced before a job can be closed (for example, device serial number and test kit)
- A full export, so you keep your history if you change platforms

## How should plumbing quotes be built?

**Fast, accurate quotes come from current supplier prices and reusable kits, not from typing each line.** Plumbing wholesalers increasingly publish price files to the main platforms; Middy's, for example, lists direct price file integration with simPRO and Fergus and CSV files for AroFlo. Build kits for repeat work (a hot water swap, a backflow device install, a TMV replacement) so each quote starts from a tested template with labour, parts and compliance fees included. Custom quoting work only makes sense when your pricing depends on rules the platform can't express.
