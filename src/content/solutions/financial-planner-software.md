## Where do the hours go in an advice practice?

**Most of an adviser's week goes on documenting advice rather than giving it: file notes after meetings, statements of advice, annual reviews and fee consents.** Each document exists to show that the advice met the client's objectives, financial situation and needs, and that the adviser acted in their best interests.

A typical client relationship generates:

| Stage | Document | Who usually prepares it |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery meeting | File note, fact find | Adviser, then client services |
| Strategy | Modelling, product research | Paraplanner |
| Recommendation | Statement of advice (SOA) | Paraplanner, adviser signs off |
| Implementation | Product applications, authorities | Client services |
| Each year | Review notes, record of advice where relevant, ongoing fee consent | Adviser and client services |
| Any time | Complaint record | Practice manager |

The bottleneck is usually the paraplanner and the turnaround from meeting to SOA. That is where software, and carefully governed AI, can save the most time.

This page is about ASIC licensed advice practices. If you are an APRA regulated bank, insurer or super fund, see our page on [AI for financial services](/solutions/ai-for-financial-services) instead.

## What do Xplan, Midwinter, AdviserLogic and Padua hold?

**The advice platform is your record of clients, fact finds, modelling and documents, and in most practices it should stay that way.** Iress describes Xplan as covering fact finds for individuals, trusts and businesses, advice workflows from onboarding to reviews, modelling, a client portal and integrations. Midwinter offers CRM, a dynamic fact find, product comparison, scaled advice, advice documents and a client portal, and publishes API documentation. AdviserLogic and Padua are other advice platforms built for the Australian market.

Licensees often choose the platform, or approve a short list. Before you build anything, find out:

1. Which API or data export your platform and licence allow.
2. Whether your licensee permits third party tools to read or write client data.
3. Whether SOA templates are locked by the licensee or editable by the practice.

Those three answers decide whether a custom tool reads from the platform, writes back to it, or sits beside it.

## What did the Delivering Better Financial Outcomes reforms change?

**Tranche 1 of the DBFO reforms, Schedule 1 of the Treasury Laws Amendment (Delivering Better Financial Outcomes and Other Measures) Act 2024, received Royal Assent on 9 July 2024 and changed fees, consents, financial services guides and conflicted remuneration.** The main paperwork changes, as ASIC summarises them:

| Change | Start |
|---|---|
| FSG information can be published on your website instead of handed over | 10 July 2024 |
| Conflicted remuneration rules clarified | 10 July 2024 |
| Fee disclosure statement removed for ongoing fee arrangements; consent content changed; more flexible anniversary timing | 10 January 2025 for new arrangements, with transition for existing ones |
| Non ongoing fee consent content changed, including advice fees from super | 10 January 2025 |
| Informed consent before accepting certain insurance commissions | 9 July 2025 |

ASIC describes the remaining Quality of Advice Review recommendations as a second tranche of legislation. Treat any future change to the best interests duty or SOA requirements as unconfirmed until it is law, and design templates so they can change without a rebuild.

## How should the ongoing fee consent work as a workflow?

**Each year a client must sign a written consent within a window that opens 60 days before the reference day and closes 150 days after it; if that doesn't happen, the arrangement terminates and fees must stop.** ASIC's INFO 286 explains that the reference day can be agreed with the client, provided it is earlier than the anniversary and disclosed before consent. Charging after termination attracts civil penalties.

The consent itself has mandatory content, including the fee recipient's details, why consent is sought, the maximum period before consent ceases, the services, an estimate of each fee and how it was calculated, the frequency, a statement that the client can terminate at any time, and the date the arrangement ends if consent isn't given.

A renewal tracker turns that into a daily list:

- Every arrangement with its reference day and window dates.
- Consents sent, opened, signed or outstanding, by adviser.
- Arrangements inside the last 30 days of the window, flagged red.
- Arrangements that lapsed, with a task to turn off fees at the product provider.
- An audit trail of what the client was sent and when they signed.

Most platforms track some of this. Practices with several advisers, or several licensee rule sets, often need it joined into one view.

## AI file notes and SOA drafts: what must the adviser check?

**AI can turn a recorded meeting into a structured file note and turn a strategy into a first draft SOA, but the adviser stays accountable for every word.** ASIC's guidance on SOAs covers required content, especially disclosure of remuneration, commissions and conflicts. Those are exactly the parts a model can get wrong.

Build the review into the tool, not around it:

1. Recording consent captured at the start of the meeting.
2. Transcript and draft file note shown side by side, with each point linked to where it was said.
3. Draft SOA generated in the licensee's template, with figures pulled from the platform rather than from the transcript.
4. Required sections checked for presence, such as fees, commissions, conflicts and the basis of advice.
5. Adviser edits tracked, approval recorded, final document filed in the platform.

ASIC's REP 798, released in October 2024, reviewed 23 AFS and credit licensees and found governance arrangements lagging AI use. Expect your licensee to ask which model processed client data, where it was hosted and how errors are caught. ## What do complaint records need to show?

**AFS licensees must have internal dispute resolution that meets ASIC's RG 271 and must be members of AFCA.** RG 271, issued in September 2021, sets out how firms record and respond to complaints and the timeframes for doing so, and most firms also report IDR data to ASIC. A complaints register linked to the client file, with dates and outcomes, gives your licensee what it needs and shows patterns early.

## Can you see the whole practice in one dashboard?

**Usually only with some joining work, because revenue, client activity and product data sit in different systems.** The platform knows clients and reviews, the product providers know balances and fees deducted, the accounting system knows what was actually received, and the consent tracker knows which arrangements are at risk.

A practice dashboard that joins those sources answers questions principals ask every month:

- Which advisers have reviews overdue, and by how many clients?
- Which ongoing fees were deducted for arrangements that have lapsed?
- How long does an SOA take from meeting to signature, by paraplanner?
- Which clients have had no contact in 12 months?

The second question matters most. Fees charged after an arrangement terminates are a remediation problem, and a nightly check that compares lapsed arrangements with provider fee data catches it early. This is [data analytics](/services/data-analytics) work rather than AI, and it often pays for itself first.

| Build | Includes | Indicative range |
