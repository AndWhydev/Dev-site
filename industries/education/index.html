Challenges We Solve

The Pain Points We Understand

The problems we plan around when building for education, because they shape the architecture from day one.

Legacy LMS Limitations

Moodle installations from 2015 and proprietary LMS platforms that pre-date mobile-first design are holding institutions back. They lack modern capabilities like adaptive learning paths, rich media streaming, micro-credentialling support, and the API surface needed to integrate with the broader edtech ecosystem. Maintaining and customising them is expensive, and the student experience suffers.

Student Engagement Decline

Online and blended learning cohorts struggle to stay engaged without the passive social reinforcement of campus life. Completion rates for online courses in Australian vocational and higher education remain well below face-to-face equivalents. The gap is partly content design, but it's also a technology problem: platforms that don't proactively surface the right content, flag at-risk students early or build community connections lose students to disengagement.

Compliance Reporting Burden

RTOs and providers receiving government funding face significant AVETMISS reporting obligations to NCVER, state training authorities and the Australian Skills Quality Authority (ASQA). Generating compliant NAT files from disparate student management systems, fixing validation errors before submission deadlines and maintaining the student records required for audit is a major administrative overhead that consumes resources that could be better spent on teaching.

Disconnected Student Data

Student information is fragmented across a student management system (SMS), LMS, library system, timetabling platform, pastoral care records and financial systems. Advisors, academics and administrators work from incomplete pictures of each student's progress and risk profile. The data needed for meaningful intervention, who is falling behind, who is at risk of not completing, exists in the organisation but can't be assembled quickly enough to act on.

FAQ, Education

The most common uses of AI in education are tutoring and study assistants grounded in approved course material, feedback drafts on student writing, marking and moderation support, lesson and resource preparation for teachers, and answering student admin questions from policy documents. For schools and RTOs, the design questions are about student privacy, age-appropriate guardrails, keeping data in Australia and making sure a teacher or assessor stays responsible for any judgement about a student. We build these tools on course content you control rather than open web search.

Education app development ranges from a focused portal or integration built in a few weeks to a multi-year platform. The main cost drivers are the number of systems to integrate (SMS, LMS, identity, finance), reporting obligations such as AVETMISS, the number of user roles and whether you need native mobile apps. We quote a fixed price after a paid discovery; our app development cost guide gives typical Australian market ranges.

Usually not. Most RTOs are better served by a commercial student management system that supports the VET Information Standard, with custom education software built only for the integrations, portals and reporting the SMS doesn't cover. Building your own SMS makes sense for large providers with unusual delivery models, and it means owning every future NCVER change yourself.

We build to the current NCVER reporting standard. AVETMISS 8 is being replaced by the VET Information Standard, with new reporting requirements commencing on 1 October 2026 and quarterly submissions to NCVER, so any system built now should target the new standard (our guide to software for Australian RTOs covers the transition). We treat compliance rule changes the same way we treat software dependencies: we track the NCVER release schedule, test against the updated validation rules in our staging environment before the effective date, and deploy updates ahead of compliance deadlines. We implement reporting validation as a separate module so it can be updated independently of the rest of the application. We also support state training authority variations (including SVTS for Victoria, Skills Gateway for NSW and CAPP for Queensland) which have requirements layered on top of the national standard.

Yes, and this is often the right approach. Both Moodle and Canvas have robust plugin and API ecosystems that allow significant extension without replacing the core platform. We build Moodle plugins (in PHP) and Canvas LTI tools (which are platform-agnostic and work in any LTI 1.3-compliant LMS) that add capabilities like custom reporting, integration with student management systems, advanced assessment workflows and interactive content types. If a replacement is warranted, we typically run the new system in parallel during a transition period rather than a hard cutover, which is less disruptive for staff and students mid-semester.

Higher education providers now report student and course data through TCSI (Tertiary Collection of Student Information), which replaced the older HEIMS and HEPCAT reporting tools. We build integrations against the published TCSI data element specifications and API, with validation against the collection rules before submission, and reconciliation reports so the data custodian team can see what was sent and what was rejected. For providers on PeopleSoft Campus Solutions, Callista or another student management system, we build the extraction and transformation layer between that system and TCSI.

LTI 1.3 is the current standard for integrating third-party tools into LMS platforms. We implement both the Tool side (building applications that embed inside Moodle, Canvas, Blackboard or D2L) and the Platform side (building custom LMS platforms that can host external LTI tools). Our LTI implementations cover the full spec: Deep Linking for content item selection, Assignment and Grades Services for grade passback, Names and Role Provisioning Services for roster sync, and the security model using signed JWTs and JWKS key rotation. We also build SCORM 1.2 and SCORM 2004 content wrappers and xAPI (Tin Can) statement producers for content tracking in an LRS.

Yes. Independent and Catholic schools often need custom technology that sits between their student management system (typically Edumate, TASS.web, Synergetic or Sentral) and the tools teachers and parents use daily. Common projects include parent portals with real-time academic progress and attendance data, custom reporting platforms that go beyond what the SMS generates natively, and integrations between the SMS and communication platforms like Schoolzine, Operoo (Care Monkey) or SchoolStream. We also help schools with the engineering side of data governance (access controls, retention and auditability), designed to the standards their privacy officer references, typically the APPs and, for Commonwealth-funded schools, the National Schools Privacy Code. Formal compliance sign-off remains with the school's governance and counsel.
