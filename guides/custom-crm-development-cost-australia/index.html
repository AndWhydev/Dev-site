How much does a custom CRM cost in Australia? (and when HubSpot or GoHighLevel is enough)

Last updated 8 November 2026 Published by AWLabs How we write

The short answer

A custom CRM built by an Australian team typically costs $20,000 to $40,000 (AUD, ex GST) for a simple CRM used by one team, $40,000 to $100,000 for a CRM with automation and a few integrations, and $100,000 to $250,000 or more for a multi team or industry specific system with portals and several integrations. Those are market ranges, not quotes. For most small businesses, HubSpot, Salesforce, Zoho or GoHighLevel is cheaper over five years and should be tried first. A custom CRM earns its cost when your sales or service process is unusual, when you would otherwise pay for many seats, or when the CRM has to run core operations, not just track leads.

Key takeaways

  • Published Australian ranges for a custom CRM run from about $20k for a basic single team system to $250k or more for a complex one (AUD, ex GST).
  • Over five years, a subscription CRM is usually cheaper for teams of around 20 or fewer users. The gap closes as seat numbers grow, because custom software doesn't charge per user.
  • Integrations, data migration and permissions drive custom CRM cost far more than the number of screens.
  • Extending the CRM you already pay for with custom integrations is often the best middle path.
  • Whatever you choose, your CRM must support Spam Act consent and unsubscribe rules and Privacy Act security obligations for the contact data it holds.

How much does a custom CRM cost in Australia?

A custom CRM built by an Australian team usually costs between $20,000 and $250,000 (AUD, ex GST), and the band is set by integrations, data migration and permissions rather than by the number of screens. A CRM that tracks contacts and a pipeline for one sales team sits at the bottom. A CRM that runs quoting, jobs, a customer portal and a two way accounting sync sits near the top.

CRM typeTypical scopeMarket range (AUD, ex GST)
Simple CRM for one teamContacts, companies, a single pipeline, notes, tasks, basic reports$20,000 to $40,000
CRM with automationSeveral pipelines, email and SMS automation, quoting, one or two integrations such as accounting or calendars$40,000 to $100,000
Operations CRM or industry systemSeveral teams with different permissions, job or case management, a customer portal, several integrations, audit trails$100,000 to $250,000+

These bands combine PerfectionGeeks’ tiers ($20k to $40k basic, $40k to $80k with automation, $80k to $150k+ enterprise), Appinventiv’s 2026 Australian range of $40,000 to $250,000, and Expeed’s general custom software bands of $15k to $40k for simple internal tools and $40k to $150k for mid complexity systems. Appinventiv also shows how industry changes the number: it puts real estate CRMs at $60,000 to $140,000 and finance CRMs at $90,000 to $250,000.

The ranges are market ranges, not quotes. Add GST, and budget roughly 15 to 20% of the build cost each year for maintenance, plus hosting. Our custom software cost guide explains the general arithmetic behind these bands.

When is HubSpot, Salesforce, Zoho or GoHighLevel enough?

An off the shelf CRM is enough when your process looks like a normal sales or service pipeline and your team is small enough that seat fees stay modest. That describes most small businesses. Try one before you commission a build.

Choose off the shelf when:

  • your sales process has standard stages and one or two pipelines;
  • you can accept the vendor’s data model and reporting;
  • integrations you need already exist in the platform’s marketplace;
  • you want to be running next month, not next quarter.

What does a CRM cost over five years: subscription or custom?

For a team of about 20, a subscription CRM is usually cheaper over five years. As the team grows, per seat fees grow with it while a custom system’s running cost barely moves. The table below uses list prices at the time of writing and the custom build from the worked example further down. It is illustrative arithmetic, not a quote.

OptionUsersFive year arithmeticFive year total (approx. AUD, before GST)
GoHighLevel StarterUnlimitedUS$97 × 60 months = US$5,820$8,300, plus usage charges
HubSpot Sales Hub Professional20US$90 × 20 seats × 60 months + US$1,500 onboarding = US$109,500$156,400
Salesforce Pro Suite20AU$140 × 20 × 60 months$168,000
Custom CRM (worked example)Any$125,400 build + 5 × $23,610 running$243,450
HubSpot Sales Hub Professional60US$90 × 60 × 60 months + US$1,500 = US$325,500$465,000
Salesforce Pro Suite60AU$140 × 60 × 60 months$504,000

Three things the table doesn’t show:

  • Setup and admin for subscriptions. Configuring a platform, migrating data and paying an administrator or partner can add tens of thousands of dollars, especially on Salesforce.
  • Workarounds. Staff time lost to re-keying, spreadsheets on the side and manual follow ups is a real cost if the platform doesn’t fit.
  • Lock in and exit. Leaving a subscription CRM means exporting data and rebuilding automations elsewhere. With custom software you own the code once it is paid for, but you are responsible for maintaining it.

So the honest reading: at 20 users with a standard process, buy. At 60 or more users, or when the CRM runs your operations, a custom build can pay for itself, and the decision turns on fit rather than price.

What drives the cost of a custom CRM?

The biggest cost drivers are integrations, migration and the number of roles with different permissions. Each adds design, build and testing work to every feature.

DriverLower costHigher cost
Users and rolesOne team, everyone sees everythingSales, service, finance, managers and customers, each with different access
Pipelines and recordsOne pipeline, standard contact and company recordsSeveral pipelines, custom records such as jobs, properties, policies or sites
IntegrationsNone, or one well documented APIAccounting, email, telephony, calendars, ad platforms, an ERP, two way sync
AutomationSimple remindersBranching sequences, approvals, SLA timers, lead routing rules
ReportingA few fixed reportsDashboards by team and user, forecasts, exports for finance
Mobile useResponsive web screensAn app for field staff that works offline
Data migrationStarting freshYears of records from spreadsheets and an old CRM, needing deduplication
ComplianceLow sensitivity contact dataHealth, financial or legal data with audit logs and tighter access control

What are the signs you’ve outgrown an off the shelf CRM?

You’ve outgrown a CRM when the team spends more effort working around it than working in it. One or two of these is normal; four or more suggests it’s time to price the alternatives.

  • Staff keep a spreadsheet beside the CRM because the CRM can’t hold the information they need.
  • The same customer details are typed into the CRM, the accounting system and a job tool.
  • You pay for a higher tier just to get one feature, such as custom objects or API access.
  • Reports are built by exporting to Excel every week.
  • Automations break whenever someone changes a field, and nobody knows how they work.
  • The vendor’s next price rise would cost more per year than a meaningful part of a build.
  • Customers ask for a portal or a self service view the platform can’t provide.

Should you extend the CRM you already have instead?

Often, yes: keeping your current CRM and building custom integrations or screens around it is the cheapest way to fix a poor fit. It suits businesses whose CRM handles contacts and email well but can’t talk to the rest of the business.

Common extensions:

  • a sync between the CRM and Xero or MYOB so invoices and payments show against the customer;
  • a custom quoting tool that writes deals back to the CRM;
  • lead routing rules the platform can’t express;
  • a customer portal that reads from the CRM;
  • automations through a platform like n8n, Make or Zapier, compared in our automation tools guide.

The trade off: you keep paying the subscription and you work within the platform’s API limits and data model. If the extensions keep growing, that is a sign the platform no longer fits.

A worked example: a custom CRM for a 20 person service business

This is a hypothetical business with illustrative figures inside the published ranges. A 20 person service company wants one system for enquiries, quotes, jobs and follow up, connected to its accounting software, replacing a basic CRM and several spreadsheets.

  1. Estimate hours by feature. Contacts and companies 60 hours; enquiry pipeline and job records 80; quoting with templates 70; two way accounting sync 100; email and SMS integration 50; dashboards and reports 60; roles and permissions 40; migrating records from the old CRM and spreadsheets 60; interface design 60; automated testing and QA 90; deployment and environments 30; project management 60. Total: 760 hours.
  2. Apply an assumed market blended rate of $165 an hour (not our rate): 760 × $165 = $125,400 ex GST.
  3. Add running costs. Maintenance at 15% of the build is $18,810 a year; hosting, email and SMS services at an assumed $400 a month is $4,800. Total: $23,610 a year, plus SMS usage.
  4. Five year total: $125,400 + (5 × $23,610) = $243,450 ex GST.

Against a subscription at 20 seats, that is more expensive, so the build only makes sense here if the quoting, job and accounting workflow is what the business actually needs and the platforms can’t do it without heavy workarounds. If the company expects to double its headcount, or the system replaces several other subscriptions, the comparison moves in the build’s favour.

How do you migrate contacts and history without losing data?

Plan the migration as its own piece of work: map every field, clean the data before you move it, and run the old and new systems side by side for a short period. Migration is where CRM projects most often slip.

  1. Export everything early, including notes, activity history, attachments and custom fields, not just contacts.
  2. Map fields from the old structure to the new one and decide what to drop.
  3. Deduplicate and fix records before import. Doing this in house is cheaper than paying developers to do it.
  4. Keep consent records. Who agreed to receive marketing, when and how must come across with the contact.
  5. Do a trial import, check a sample by hand, then do the final import.
  6. Freeze the old system to read only once the new one is live, rather than deleting it straight away.

What Australian rules must a CRM support?

Your CRM holds personal information and sends marketing, so it has to support the Privacy Act and the Spam Act whichever option you choose. This is general information, not legal advice.

  • Spam Act 2003. The ACMA says you need consent before sending marketing emails or SMS, you must identify yourself as the sender, and every message must make it easy to unsubscribe. It also says it is up to you to prove consent, so record who gave it, when and how.
  • Privacy Act 1988, APP 11. If your business is covered by the Act, you must take reasonable steps to protect personal information from misuse, interference, loss and unauthorised access, and destroy or de-identify it when it is no longer needed. In a CRM that means sensible roles, audit logs, backups and a retention rule.
  • Where the data is stored. Check which region a subscription CRM hosts your data in. A custom CRM can be hosted in an Australian cloud region, which matters when your customer data needs to stay onshore.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a custom CRM cost in Australia?

Published Australian guides put a basic CRM for a small business at about $20,000 to $40,000 (AUD), a CRM with automation at $40,000 to $80,000, and enterprise level systems from $80,000 to $250,000 or more. Integrations, data migration and the number of user roles decide where yours lands. Add hosting and maintenance of roughly 15 to 20% of the build cost each year.

Is it worth building your own CRM?

It is worth it when an off the shelf CRM forces your team into workarounds every day, when the CRM is really your operations system (jobs, quotes, bookings, compliance records), or when per seat fees for a large team exceed the cost of owning software. For a small sales team with a standard pipeline, it usually isn't worth it: a subscription is cheaper and faster.

Custom CRM vs HubSpot: which is better?

HubSpot is better for marketing led businesses that want email, forms, landing pages and a sales pipeline in one place, and can live with its data model. A custom CRM is better when your records, workflow or permissions don't fit HubSpot's objects without heavy customisation, or when seat costs at your scale add up to more than a build. Many businesses keep HubSpot and add custom integrations instead.

GoHighLevel vs a custom CRM: which should I choose?

GoHighLevel is priced per account with unlimited users at the time of writing, so it is very cheap for small teams that need pipelines, SMS, email and booking in one tool, and it suits agencies running many client accounts. A custom CRM makes sense when you need workflows, data structures or integrations GoHighLevel can't model, or you want the software and data fully under your control.

What is the best CRM for a small business in Australia?

There isn't one best CRM. A useful rule: HubSpot or Zoho for a small sales or marketing team, GoHighLevel for businesses that live on SMS, calls and bookings, Salesforce when you expect complex sales processes and a larger team, and a job management platform rather than a CRM for trades and field service. Trial two or three with your real data before committing.

How long does it take to build a custom CRM?

Published Australian guides put mid complexity business systems at about 8 to 16 weeks, while Appinventiv puts custom CRMs at 10 to 12 months for more complex builds. A first release that covers contacts, pipeline, quoting and one integration can often go live in a few months, with further features added in stages.

Can you build a custom CRM on top of HubSpot or Salesforce?

Yes. Both platforms have APIs and support custom objects and apps, so a common approach is to keep the platform for contacts and email and build custom screens, integrations or automations around it. It costs less than a full build, but you keep paying the subscription and stay within the platform's limits.

How much does a custom CRM cost to run each year?

In this guide's worked example, about $23,610 a year ex GST for a $125,400 build: maintenance at 15% of the build ($18,810) plus an assumed $400 a month for hosting, email and SMS services. SMS usage is extra. As a planning rule, allow 15 to 20% of the build cost a year for maintenance, then add hosting and messaging on top.

Is a custom CRM cheaper than HubSpot or Salesforce for a large team?

Often, yes, once the team is large. In this guide's five year arithmetic at 60 users, HubSpot Sales Hub Professional comes to about $465,000 and Salesforce Pro Suite about $504,000, against roughly $243,450 for the worked example custom CRM. At 20 users the subscriptions are cheaper. Custom software doesn't charge per user, so the gap widens as headcount grows.

Can a custom CRM include a customer portal and job management?

Yes, and that is where a custom CRM most often earns its cost. An operations CRM with several teams on different permissions, job or case management, a customer portal, several integrations and audit trails typically costs $100,000 to $250,000 or more (AUD, ex GST). A CRM with automation and one or two integrations sits at $40,000 to $100,000.

These figures are market ranges, not quotes and not what AWLabs charges. Every AWLabs build is priced after our Discovery Audit.
