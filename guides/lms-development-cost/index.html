How much does LMS development cost in Australia?

Last updated 8 November 2026 Published by AWLabs How we write

The short answer

Most Australian organisations should start with a SaaS LMS, which costs roughly US$1,500 to US$6,000 a year at published prices for small and mid sized audiences, or hosted Moodle through a partner. A custom LMS makes sense when learning is the product you sell or must sit inside your own platform. Worked at an assumed agency day rate of $1,100 to $1,500, a custom course platform with payments, certificates and CPD tracking lands around $60k to $150k (AUD, ex GST), and an enterprise LMS with single sign on and HR sync $150k to $400k or more. These are market ranges and worked examples, not quotes.

Key takeaways

  • There are three paths: open source (Moodle), SaaS (TalentLMS, LearnWorlds, LearnUpon) and custom. Most employers should not build.
  • SaaS pricing is usually per active user or per enrolment, so it gets expensive when you have a large, occasional audience such as members or customers.
  • A custom LMS earns its cost when courses are what you sell, when learning must live inside your own app, or when your reporting rules are unusual.
  • Content standards (SCORM, xAPI, cmi5), certificates and CPD rules are separate cost lines. Scope them explicitly.
  • Accredited training brings USI and AVETMISS reporting, which changes the build. Keep that decision separate from the LMS choice.

Should you buy, host or build an LMS?

Buy a SaaS LMS if you are training your own staff; host Moodle if you want control without a build; build custom only when learning is your product or must live inside your own platform. That one decision moves the budget more than any feature list.

Most Australian employers should not build an LMS. The SaaS market is mature, prices are published, and the hard parts (content players, completion tracking, reminders) are solved. Our build vs buy guide sets out the general test.

What do SaaS LMS platforms charge, and when does it get expensive?

SaaS LMS pricing is low for a stable, known audience and climbs quickly when the audience is large, occasional or outside your organisation. Published prices at the time of writing (November 2026):

VendorPricing modelPublished price
TalentLMSMonthly active users, billed yearly or monthly, USDCore from US$119 a month; Grow US$229; Pro US$449, with a per user rate above the plan limit
LearnWorldsPlan fee, plus an enrolment fee on the entry plan, USDStarter 49 a month (44 on annual billing) plus $5 per course enrolment; Pro Trainer 99 (79 annual); Learning Center 299 (249 annual)
LearnUponQuote based, plans quoted from 100, 150 or 300 usersNot published; says it is not a fit below 100 users
MoodleCloudHosted Moodle in tiers by user countOwn domain is an extra $160 AUD a year on the Medium plan; plugins cannot be installed

The pattern to watch is the audience. An employer with 200 staff who all log in most months pays a predictable fee. An association with 8,000 members, of whom 1,500 take one course a year, either pays for capacity it rarely uses or pays per enrolment on top. A course business selling 2,000 enrolments a year on an entry plan pays $10,000 in enrolment fees alone, which is why LearnWorlds nudges growing sellers to a higher tier.

Currency matters too. Most of these vendors bill in US dollars, so your cost moves with the exchange rate, and GST may be charged on top as an imported service.

What does hosted Moodle cost to run?

Moodle itself has no licence fee because it is open source; the cost is hosting, configuration, plugins and the people who keep it upgraded. For organisations that want ownership of their learning data without commissioning a build, it is often the middle path.

Expect three cost lines. First, setup: branding, course structure, roles, single sign on and a few plugins, typically done by a Moodle partner. Second, hosting and support: either MoodleCloud for small sites (with no plugins allowed) or a partner hosted site with a support agreement. Third, upgrades: Moodle releases major versions regularly, and every plugin you add must be checked against each one. That last line is where Moodle budgets drift.

As planning figures, not quotes, a partner configured Moodle with branding and two or three integrations is often scoped in the $10k to $40k range, with hosting and support at $3k to $15k a year depending on users and service level. Get two partner quotes before you rely on either number.

What does a custom LMS cost to build?

Worked at an assumed agency rate of $1,100 to $1,500 a day, a custom LMS ranges from about $60k for a focused course platform to $400k or more for an enterprise system. The rate assumption reflects Re:Sourced’s July 2026 guide, which puts senior software contractors in Sydney at $800 to $1,100 a day ex GST; agencies charge more to cover design, testing and project management. Conduct’s 2026 guide puts educational software at roughly $15k for basic tools, $80k for medium complexity and $150k or more for advanced platforms, which is consistent with these bands.

The custom path costs more on these numbers. It pays back only if course sales are meaningful revenue, if SaaS cannot express the CPD rules your board requires, or if you are building a learning product to sell to other organisations. In that last case, read our SaaS development cost guide.

Which content standards and course features add cost?

SCORM, xAPI and cmi5 decide whether packaged content from authoring tools will play and report correctly, and each one is a scoped line item, not a checkbox. SCORM 1.2 and SCORM 2004 are what most authoring tools still export, and a solid player with completion and score tracking takes about a week to build and test. xAPI records richer activity statements but needs a learning record store. cmi5 combines xAPI with launch rules similar to SCORM. If your organisation writes all content inside the platform, you may need none of them.

Other features that move the number:

  • Certificates with verification links, expiry and renewal reminders.
  • CPD tracking that mirrors a regulator’s or board’s categories and caps. Our membership and association software page covers how CPD rules vary.
  • Assessments beyond multiple choice: file uploads, marking queues, moderation.
  • Accessibility: captions, transcripts and keyboard access for every lesson type.

How do payments and GST work when you sell courses?

If you are registered for GST, courses sold to Australian customers generally include 10% GST, while the ATO says exports of services from Australia are generally GST free. The checkout must work out which applies, issue a valid tax invoice and pass the right figures to your accounting system. The rules for overseas buyers have conditions, so confirm the treatment with your accountant before launch.

If you sell courses as a subscription or membership, Australia’s new subscription rules apply from 1 July 2027. Our guide to building a subscription app covers disclosure and cancellation requirements.

What do AI features add?

AI tutoring, quiz drafting and feedback on written answers add build effort and a running cost per learner. Drafting questions from course material is cheap to add and easy to review. A tutor that answers questions from your content is a retrieval system with its own testing and monitoring, typically several weeks of work. Automated marking of written answers that affect certification needs human review built in. Budget model usage as a monthly running cost that grows with active learners.

What changes for accredited training?

Registered training organisations need more than an LMS: they must verify Unique Student Identifiers and report AVETMISS data, which is the job of a student management system. The USI Registry offers web services that student management systems integrate with. Most RTOs run a specialist student management system alongside an LMS rather than building both. Our RTO and education software page covers those obligations, and the education industry page covers the wider sector.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to build an LMS?

As a worked example at an assumed agency rate of $1,100 to $1,500 a day, a custom course platform with payments, certificates and CPD tracking takes roughly 55 to 100 days, or about $60k to $150k ex GST. An enterprise LMS with single sign on, HR system sync, compliance reporting and AI features is typically $150k to $400k or more. Hosted Moodle configured by a partner is far cheaper to start.

What does an LMS cost per user?

At the time of writing, TalentLMS publishes plans from US$119 a month on yearly billing, priced on the number of unique users who log in each month, and charges a per user rate above the plan limit. LearnWorlds charges a monthly plan fee and, on its Starter plan, a fee of $5 per course enrolment. Per user pricing works well for a stable staff audience and poorly for large, occasional audiences.

Is Moodle free?

Moodle LMS is open source, so there is no licence fee for the software. You still pay for hosting, plugins, upgrades and support, usually through a Moodle partner. MoodleCloud is Moodle's own hosted option for smaller sites, but it does not allow plugins to be installed.

Do we charge GST on online courses?

Generally yes for Australian customers if you are registered for GST. The ATO says exports of services from Australia are generally GST free, which can cover courses sold to overseas buyers, but the rules have conditions. Check your situation with your accountant before you set up the checkout.

Do we need SCORM?

Only if you buy or exchange packaged course content with other systems. SCORM 1.2 and 2004 are still what most authoring tools export. xAPI and cmi5 record richer activity, but need a learning record store. If all content is written inside your own platform, you may not need any of them.

Can a custom LMS handle accredited training for an RTO?

It can, but an RTO also needs a student management system that reports USI and AVETMISS data, and that is usually a separate product. Our RTO software page covers those obligations; most RTOs integrate an LMS with a specialist student management system rather than building both.
